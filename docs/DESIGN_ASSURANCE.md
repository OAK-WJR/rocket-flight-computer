# Rocket Flight Computer v1 — Design Assurance Plan

> Inputs: `ROCKET_FC_FROZEN_DESIGN.md`, the as-drawn netlist (78 nets / 370 connections / 104 references, machine-checked this round) and the schematic notes.
> Status at the time of writing: schematic captured, **PCB not yet laid out**. Every "before fab" action in this plan was still free at that point.

---

## 1. Direct answers to the three questions

**(1) Manufacturing errors.** You are not a factory: no inspection line, 5 boards, through-hole parts soldered by hand. So the strategy is not "add inspection" but **delete the error's degrees of freedom from the design, and catch the rest with an unpowered DC signature table**. This board is unusually rich in unpowered evidence: every safety-critical node has a unique resistor or diode signature readable with a $20 multimeter on an unpowered board — each of J5's four signal pins reads 10.22 kΩ to ground (`R24 + R28`) and no other pin reads that value; each pyro FET drain reads 11.0 kΩ (`R18 + R19`), a shorted part < 1 kΩ; each pyro gate reads 1.00 kΩ (`R17` / `R21`) — the only thing holding PE4/PE5 down at power-up; `GND → BAT+` in diode mode reads OL both ways, proving the bidirectional SMBJ12CA was fitted rather than a unidirectional SMBJ12A that would short a 25C LiPo. **Add one rule: every board's first power-up is always USB first, then a current-limited bench supply, never a battery first.** On USB, `RAW ≈ 0 V` (D2 reverse-biased) and there is no 8.4 V on the servo header at all — this rule downgrades a wrong J5 numbering, a reversed polymer can, a `BAT+/BAT−` bridge and a reversed SOT-23-6 array from "fire" to "supply goes into constant current". The only place worth spending money: **buy JLC's through-hole soldering** (about $4 per order, *unverified whether the Economic tier offers it*), which removes the project's two most dangerous assembly defects (a cold joint on J1's SW pins, bridges on J1/J4) by prevention rather than detection. If it cannot be bought, give every plane-connected through-hole pad thermal relief, otherwise a hand iron cannot heat through a GND pin tied to inner planes on a 4-layer 1.6 mm board.

**(2) Environment.** Quantify the environment first, then **refuse half of what is usually done**. Amateur high-power flight has only 0.5–4 s of boost vibration — 200–800 stress cycles at a 200 Hz first mode, about 12,000 cycles over 20 flights. Steinberg's 0.355 mm allowable displacement is a **2 × 10⁷-cycle** criterion; scaled to 600 cycles with his own fatigue exponent b = 6.4 it is about 1.8 mm, against an actual 3σ displacement of 0.352 mm → **about 5× margin**. So: **no mid-span support, no potting the board to its bracket, no RTV on the two 470 µF cans**. The board's real environmental enemies, in order: landing and deployment shock (single overloads: 92 g @ 20 mm crush / 367 g @ 5 mm on hard ground / 1593 g ballistic; GEVS Fig 2.4-1's benign box is 500 g), **the connector/harness interface** (deployment shock pulls directly on the DB128V screw clamps), and **a hot bay nobody has measured** (MIL-STD-810H Method 501.7 §2.3.2 enclosed-compartment intensified solar: 71–85 °C). The three corresponding actions are all cheap: ferrules + torque-stripe paint, a zip-tie hole on each side of J5 to hold down the four plug housings, and writing the MS5611 temperature word into every 1 Hz record. **Conformal coating: not done** — reasons in §6. *(MARGIN_PRESCRIPTION.md later overturned two of these: staking of the 470 µF cans and conformal coating are now mandatory.)*

**(3) Errors in the design itself — this is the centre of gravity, and redundancy is structurally useless here.** All four servos get the same wrong sign; all five boards use the same wrong footprint pad mapping; both pyro channels run through the same wrong apogee code. Redundancy copies **the same error**. Only three things actually work, in order of effectiveness:

- **Before spending money, run a deterministic procedure that checks what ERC/DRC structurally cannot see.** ERC/DRC work only on the netlist graph; they do not know what a footprint looks like or what a datasheet says. The three items in the schematic notes (J5 rows/columns, LS1's second terminal, MS5611 SDO) all got in **because someone happened to notice**, not because a process caught them. Make it a process: export pad coordinates for the 11 parts whose "symbol pin number cannot be inferred visually from the footprint", and check them against the manufacturer's pin table (**not against the schematic symbol — the symbol is the thing under test**). 30 minutes. Using the same method this round found three new defects in an hour (below).
- **Let wrong assumptions be falsified by physics, not confirmed by your own tests.** A test corpus you synthesised yourself carries the same wrong mental model you had while writing the code — it just moves the common-mode error up one level. The only thing that can falsify it is **real data**: a first flight as a pure data logger, arming plug removed, no igniters, servos unplugged, a commercial altimeter with sole deployment authority. Transonic pressure anomalies, gyro bias temperature drift, the closed-bay thermal curve and the real vibration spectrum only have ground truth in flight.
- **Make what cannot be made redundant structurally impossible to get wrong.** A single choke-point function `pyro_may_fire()` (CI greps and forbids any other write to PE4/PE5), a hardware timeout on the 20 ms fire pulse (fully decoupled from the IMU), apogee detection and the state machine as pure functions that compile on a host.

### Ranking

**Tier 1 — redundancy completely useless; consequence is the vehicle + people**

1. **Control-loop signs: gyro Z-axis sign × servo direction × board mounting orientation.** Zero words about it in three documents. This is an actively roll-controlled rocket whose controller integrates gyro rate; if any sign is reversed it is positive feedback, and all four fins go hard-over within a fraction of a second after lift-off. Completely silent on the bench (feedback is zero while the board is still). All four servos get the same wrong sign — a textbook common-mode design error. **Probability no lower than 1 in 4; consequence the vehicle plus an unpredictable landing point.**
2. **Transonic pressure anomaly triggering deployment.** §7 rule 7 makes the barometer the primary apogee source and says nothing about Mach. At Mach 0.8–1.2 the pressure at the static port is not free-stream pressure; the classic result is a huge false altitude jump, which a naive dP/dt sign-flip test reads as apogee — deploying at maximum dynamic pressure. **The hobby's most famous environment-induced failure**, and redundancy is useless: every redundant barometer sees the same false pressure.
3. **Row/column numbering of the J5 3×4 header (known, unresolved).** If the footprint is row-major: physical columns 1 and 4 happen to be right, column 2 = (RAW, GND, SIG4), column 3 = (GND, SIG3, RAW). **The consequence was underestimated last round**: with a servo plugged in normally, 8.4 V lands on its signal pin while its own V+ is at board ground — current flows through the servo's internal signal-input ESD clamp diode to ground, a path that **does not pass through the 220R** (the 220R is in the servo signal branch; the fault current does not use it), and RAW has **no fuse at all** (the 5 A fuse is in the arming plug and protects only the pyro branch). An unfused 25C 2S LiPo → 2.54 mm header → a servo ESD diode, in a closed electronics bay. **That is fire-class, not "the servo broke".** And it **half works** — a bench test with one servo plugged into column 1 passes.
4. **Symbol ↔ footprint pad-number mapping errors (J5 is only one instance).** Same class: LS1 (4 pads, only pin 1 named; which two are the electrical terminals is unchecked), LED1/LED2 (opposite anode numbering), D6 (is SOD-323 pin 1 the cathode?), Q1/Q2 (is the DFN5×6-8 exposed pad numbered 9 and is it the drain — the netlist uses `.9`, and that pad carries 12 A), U4/U5/U6, J1/J4 (which physical screw position is pad 1), Y1, J3 (USB-C A/B pad naming). **ERC and DRC are structurally blind to this class**, because both work on symbol pin numbers, and the symbol ↔ footprint mapping is exactly what neither checks. Identically wrong on all five boards.

**Tier 2 — single point, silent, only shows up in flight**

5. **Cold joints/bridges on hand-soldered through-hole parts.** The most dangerous is `J1`'s SW pin: `SW_TERM` carries only 76 µA (`R2` = 10k), so a marginal joint conducts perfectly on the bench with no self-heating and no voltage drop to reveal it; vibration and 20–30 g axial load pull it open → the back-to-back load switch turns off → the whole board loses power with only 18.2 ms of hold-up on `VLOGIC`. Next most dangerous: a bridge between `J1` BAT+/BAT− (hidden under the terminal body) and bridges between adjacent `J4` positions.
6. **The IWDG's single feed point (IMU DRDY ISR) + §7 rule 4 (never auto-re-arm after reset) = any watchdog reset in the air guarantees no deployment.** Each rule is right on its own; coupled they are fatal: any 2 s ISR stall in flight → MCU reset → powers up DISARMED per rule 4 → a healthy, living flight computer, deliberately forbidden by itself, rides a ballistic recovery. And with no backup record, afterwards you cannot tell "the charge didn't go" from "the computer refused to fire".
7. **The 20 ms fire pulse is only accounted for in an ISR; there is no hardware timeout.** Fire decisions run in the DRDY ISR (rule 1). If the IMU stops exactly during a pulse, the gate stays high until the watchdog reset ~2 s later — 2 s × about 5 A through a SOT-23. The device is destroyed, and it is an ignition source.
8. **`PYRO*_CONT` gives zero coverage of the switching element.** The divider only looks at the drain. Any open in the gate chain (cold joint on Q3 pin 1, cold joint on `R16`/`R17`, a FET that went open after a previous overcurrent, a firmware pin misconfiguration, a footprint G/S swap) still reads a normal 0.764 V. **The on-board self-test certifies a dead channel as healthy** — the worst class of defect.
9. **An igniter wired into an ARM position (counting from the wrong end).** The 6-pos terminal is ARM/ARM/E1/E1/E2/E2: six identical screw positions distinguished only by silkscreen, and nearly a palindrome. The real mechanism is **not an off-by-one** (every off-by-one on J4 was walked through and all are benign: pins 2/3/5 are on the same net, and landing on PYRO_BUS is harmless) but **counting from the wrong end**: mapping E2's 5→6 onto physical 2→1 = PYRO_BUS→RAW, so the igniter becomes the arming link itself, sitting on always-live RAW, while the arming plug sits across PYRO2_OUT and PYRO_BUS. The channel still fires normally, so it is never exposed; the 5 A fuse is in the wrong branch. `ARM_SENSE` and both `CONT` lines read a normal 0.764 V. **Updated consequence**: at fire time the igniter in the ARM position and the one on E1 go off together; the burnt bridgewire then permanently cuts PYRO_BUS's only supply → **the main never fires**.
10. **No on-pad gyro bias re-calibration + closed-bay self-heating.** Bias is taken on a cool board at power-up; the board then self-heats 10–20 °C while waiting on the pad (the review's own 5–15 °C self-heating estimate + sun), the ICM-45686 bias drifts ~0.5–1 °/s, and the controller faithfully commands a steady roll to cancel a rotation that does not exist. Silent, not on any checklist, and every board drifts the same way.

**Tier 3 — high probability but bounded consequence, or high consequence but low probability**

11. A string of MS5611 items (all from the datasheet, none considered in the design): **only 66 % of the offset recovers within 7 days after reflow** (absolute accuracy in the first week cannot be trusted and must not be stored as calibration); **full error band −20..85 °C is ±3.5 mbar = ±29 m** (a 10–20 % error on a 150–300 m main-deployment altitude); **450 mbar full-accuracy lower limit**; **no cleaning**; **no static-port specification at all** (an unvented bay means the barometer reads bay pressure; sealed at 20 °C and sun-heated to 60 °C, ideal-gas behaviour reads about 14 % high).
12. **The pull-pin fails in the wrong direction**: open = 3.30 V = "pin pulled, enabled". Cold joints, broken wires and corrosion all read as enabled. The final flight-enable interlock silently does not exist.
13. **DFU jumper left on the board or working loose.** `J2 pin 5 = 3V3` is next to `pin 6 = BOOT0`, BOOT0's VIH is only 1.16 V, and firmware cannot read BOOT0 — the board just fails to boot. The LEDs are invisible in a closed bay; the only detector is the buzzer.
14. **Counterfeit / relabelled parts** (AO3400A, AON6403, ICM-45686, USBLC6, MS5611, AP63203). The probability is kept low by "the C number is the brand choice" (relabels are different SKUs), but an AO3400A error covers the entire "inserting the arming plug cannot self-fire" argument.
15. **Mechanical failure of the two Ø8 × 10 mm polymer cans + the screw terminals** — from **landing shock** (92–367 g on a 2 g cantilever with its centre of mass 5.5 mm up, standing on two terminations) and **deployment pull**, not vibration fatigue.
16. **The VCAP 2.2 µF X5R falls out of ST's window at 85 °C**: 2.2 × 0.90 (tolerance) × 0.85 (X5R temperature) × 0.95 (ageing) = 1.60 µF < ST's 1.76 µF minimum, and it happens when the die is hottest. Both VCAPs degrade together, no redundancy. *Temperature/ageing factors are generic values; Samsung's actual curves not checked.*
17. **Document / reference / dimension inconsistencies causing ordering errors** ((j)(k)(l) above).
18. **LC peaking near the buck's PFM burst frequency**: the soft-start argument holds (ωn·T = 2π · 12800 · 0.004 = 322, residual ramp overshoot about 0.6 % ≈ 20 mV, no threat to 3.6 V), but **soft start does not govern light-load PFM bursts**, whose repetition rate at tens of mA falls in the kHz range and can sit on the 12.8 kHz resonance continuously; Q = 4.8 gives +13.6 dB. The consequence is analog noise on VREF+/sensors, not a dead board.
19. **Black-powder residue surface leakage to the pyro gate — ruling: bought out by the 1k pull-down, recorded and accepted.** Recomputed with the corrected `Vgs(th)` minimum of 0.65 V, it takes a surface-insulation failure of about **12 kΩ** to lift the gate to threshold; real SIR failures are in the megohm range. No parts or process spent on it. *(Superseded by MARGIN_PRESCRIPTION.md §4.13: conformal coating is now mandatory.)*

## 4. Changes that make errors discoverable

### 4.1 Before fab: a footprint pin map (30 minutes; the best-value half hour)

For every part whose "symbol pin number cannot be inferred visually from the footprint", export each pad's **number + (x, y) centre** from the EDA tool, sort by y then x, render as an ASCII grid with the assigned net beside each pad, and commit it to the repository. **Check against the manufacturer's datasheet pin table, not against the schematic symbol — the symbol is the thing under test.**

Mandatory list (11): `J1`, `J4` (which physical screw position is pad 1), `J5`, `J3` (USB-C A/B pad naming), `Q1`/`Q2` (does the DFN5×6-8 have an exposed pad numbered 9, and is it the drain — the netlist uses `.9` and that pad carries 12 A), `U4`, `U5`, `LS1`, `D6` (is SOD-323 pin 1 the cathode), `LED1`/`LED2`, `Y1`.

### 4.2 Unpowered DC signature table (every board, before power-up)

All values come from the as-drawn netlist and were checked one by one. The nine items marked ★ are the **safety subset: every board, every time, < 5 minutes, never waived**.

**Power and protection**
- `3V3–GND` > 100 kΩ ★; `3V3A–GND` > 100 kΩ; `VLOGIC–GND` > 100 kΩ
- `3V3–3V3A`: **< 1 Ω and not OL** (L2 present). *Do not* try to read the 78 mΩ DCR with a handheld meter — probe resistance of 0.1–0.3 Ω is 2–4× the quantity being measured. To close the unverified u-blox 0.2 Ω limit item, pass 300 mA through the sensor rail and read across L2 on the 200 mV range: 300 mA × 78 mΩ = 23 mV, readable on a 4½-digit meter
- `RAW–GND` = 145.5–148.5 kΩ (`R3 + R4`). Reading 104.7 kΩ → `R4` fitted as 4.7k. **The mechanism is a wrong reel/feeder or a BOM/CPL misalignment (0402s have no marking, so "misread marking" does not exist), so it is a whole-batch defect — check board 1 once; if board 1 is right, boards 2–5 very probably are too**
- Diode mode `GND → BAT+` **OL both ways** ★. Any ~0.7 V reading = a unidirectional SMBJ12A was fitted, **stop**. The cheapest discriminator in the table: a unidirectional TVS becomes a forward-biased diode on battery reversal and shorts an unfused 25C LiPo, and the two part numbers share package and price and differ by one letter
- `BAT+–RAW` OL both ways (back-to-back FETs blocking); diode mode `BAT+ → FET_S` and `RAW → FET_S` each 0.35–0.60 V, OL in reverse

### 4.3 Test points (all on the bottom side)

The bottom side is empty by design, has no fitted parts, faces the sled gap and carries no harness — so test points cost **zero top-side area, zero height and zero placement cost**. Form: **1.5 × 1.5 mm bare copper pads with a 1.8 mm solder-mask opening** (not 1.0 mm vias — probes slip and spring clips won't hold), each with a ≥ 0.8 mm silkscreen net name.

- **Group A (power, in a row along the long edge opposite J4, y ≈ 12–32, 3.0 mm pitch)**: `TP-GNDLOOP` (a 1.0 mm wire loop, not a flat pad, so the black lead stays clipped on and only one probe moves), `TP-3V3`, `TP-3V3A`, `TP-VLOGIC`, `TP-RAW`, `TP-BATP`, `TP-FETS` (Q1/Q2 common source, within 3 mm), `TP-GATESW`, `TP-SWNODE` (+ a GND via within 4 mm)
  - `TP-3V3` vs `TP-3V3A`: one measurement diagnoses an open L2 — which silently kills all three sensors while the MCU stays fine
  - `TP-BATP` vs `TP-RAW`: one measurement diagnoses the load switch and external switch
  - `TP-GATESW` − `TP-FETS` = Vgs; criterion −7.2..−8.0 V at 8.4 V, −5.2..−5.7 V at 6.0 V — the only direct verification of the 100k/10k change (the reason it was changed from 100k/100k)
- **Group B (pyro, next to Q3/Q4, ≥ 10 mm from group A)**: `TP-PY1G`, `TP-PY2G` (**must be taken on the FET side of the 470R, not the MCU side**), one `TP-GND` within 10 mm of both, `TP-CONT1`, `TP-CONT2`, `TP-ARMS`
  - `TP-CONT*` / `TP-ARMS` are divider midpoints with 909 Ω source impedance — shorting them to ground does nothing, so they are safe probe nodes, and they carry the same information as the drains
- **Never place**: `TP-PYRO_BUS`, `TP-PYRO1_OUT`, `TP-PYRO2_OUT`. Reason: putting 8.4 V PYRO_BUS 2–3 mm from a pyro gate manufactures the very injury path you are trying to prevent — a slipping probe or a dropped lead across two adjacent pads fires the channel, in exactly the bench session where the arming plug is cycled 20 times. The drains are already the `J4.4`/`J4.6` screw heads; probe the screw heads. RAW is already in group A, ≥ 10 mm from group B
- **Cost and limits**: all on the bottom = **unreachable once installed** (sled standoff ≥ 3 mm). A deliberate trade: test points on the bench, terminal screw heads once installed. No exposed test points on top, so no forgettable "apply Kapton before every flight" procedure. All 16 of the 1.8 mm openings fall in the y < 48 mm power and pyro zones and **stay out of L2's GPS reference area** — this must go into the PCB pipeline constraints, or the geometry review won't know

### 4.4 On-board self-test (firmware, zero parts)

- **Board identity from the STM32H7 96-bit UID (`0x1FF1E800`)**: firmware stores a five-row table (UID → board serial + assembly revision), refuses to leave DISARMED for an unknown UID, and writes the raw UID and decoded result into the log header. **Zero parts, zero pins, zero area, and it identifies "this board" rather than "this revision"** — exactly what per-board acceptance cards, per-board buzzer resonance, per-board S7 results and post-incident investigation need. (**Rejected**: a resistor-divider version code — it spends an ADC pin plus an exception argument against frozen pin constraints to solve a smaller problem.)
- **Flash acceptance by quad-mode readback, not JEDEC ID.** A `0x9F` ID read runs in 1-1-1 mode using only IO0/IO1 and still returns a perfect `EF 40 18` with `QSPI_IO2` (PE2) and `QSPI_IO3` (PD13) completely disconnected; and the design **deliberately deleted the WP/HOLD pull-ups**, so those lines float when open. Method: read ID → set QE → write one 256-byte LFSR page to a reserved scratch page in 1-4-4 → read back in 4-4-4 and compare byte by byte. At every boot, and also on the first page of every log session
- **Four-way ICM-45686 identification** (all free, and they verify the solder joints at the same time): (a) `WHO_AM_I` register `0x72` = `0xE9`; (b) **IREG indirect-register loopback** — a relabelled 42688 uses BANK_SEL and has no IREG path, which it cannot fake; (c) set ODR = 800 Hz and timestamp 8192 consecutive DRDYs on the free-running TIM2 microsecond timebase; the mean must be 1250 µs ± 3 % — the 42688 has **no 800 Hz step**, and this also independently verifies the clock tree; (d) **six-face 16384 LSB/g ± 5 % check**: rest the board on each of its six faces once; the downward axis in the 20-bit FIFO packet must read 16384 LSB with the correct sign and the other two axes near zero. This one action proves the range is TDK's, all 14 LGA joints conduct, and **the firmware's sensor-axis → airframe mapping** (the sign in §2 item 1). One more free relabel discriminator: flat minus inverted must be 2 × 16384 — a relabelled ±16 g part reads double. *The facts in (a)–(c) come from frozen §7.6 and were not independently checked against TDK's datasheet; check before writing firmware. (d) and the flat/inverted difference do not depend on them.*
- **MS5611**: PROM CRC-4 (already in §7 rule 7) + three additions: reject `0x0000`/`0xFFFF` in C1..C6 or all six equal (counterfeits often return constants); take 100 samples at rest at OSR = 4096 with a standard deviation **expected ≤ 0.015 mbar, fail > 0.020** (datasheet RMS resolution MS5611 = 0.012 mbar, MS5607 = 0.024 mbar — this genuinely separates the two); **cross-check three temperatures within ± 5 °C** (MCU die, IMU, MS5611). Plus a zero-cost ambiguity closure: **probe both 0x76 and 0x77 at boot, use whichever answers, and log it** — the TE datasheet's text ("the complement of CSB is the address LSB") contradicts its Figs 10–13 (address = `111011` + CSB), and the frozen document picked 0x77 unilaterally
- **Buzzer first at power-up**: a reversed D6 collapses the 3V3 rail the first time the buzzer sounds (it becomes a forward-biased diode from 3V3 to Q5's drain, once per 2.7 kHz cycle). **So the buzzer must sound in the first five minutes of bring-up**, not be left to the end
- **Log header record (type 0x01, first record of each session)**: magic, log format version, firmware git SHA-1 (20 bytes), dirty-tree flag, build timestamp, configuration-block CRC32, raw board UID and decoded serial, **raw `RCC_RSR`**, boot counter, `VREFIN_CAL` readback. **The first things to look at afterwards are `RCC_RSR` and the boot counter** — a boot count > 1 means a reset in flight, the only evidence separating "the charge didn't go" from "the computer refused to fire"
- **A two-level fault black box** (correcting a widespread error: tying `VBAT` to `VDD` does **not** remove the backup domain — it only removes retention when VDD is lost; the 32 TAMP backup registers and 4 KB backup SRAM are not cleared by system reset / IWDG reset / software reset / hard fault, and ordinary SRAM is not cleared by a warm reset either, only by the startup code's .bss zeroing, from which a noinit section is exempt). **Level 1**: in the fault handler, write the stacked frame, CFSR/HFSR/BFAR/MMFAR, the flight-phase variable and a magic into a noinit SRAM struct (or TAMP backup registers), then `NVIC_SystemReset`. A dozen store instructions, no drivers, no clock dependency, no failure mode. **Level 2**: very early at the next boot, if the magic is present, move it calmly into a QSPI record sector with the normal drivers, then clear the magic. **Never do a QSPI page program in the fault handler** — that picks the longest, most state-dependent, most hang-prone operation on a machine that has just proven itself untrustworthy
- **Power-up firmware image CRC32 self-check** (written to a fixed address at link time): on mismatch refuse to leave DISARMED and sound a specific tone code. Turns "a download interrupted by a surge → running garbage on the pad" into "refuses to run"

### 4.6 Bench console: use RTT over SWD; don't try to bring out SWO

`TRACESWO` on the STM32H7 is **PB3**, and PB3 is already `SPI1_SCK` for the ICM-45686 (netlist: `SPI1_SCK = {U1.89, U4.13}`). The full trace port PE2–PE6 is also taken by `QSPI_IO2`, both LEDs and the pyro/buzzer gates. **So the answer to "should SWO be brought out" is: it can't be, and it isn't needed.** Flight diagnostics come from the 16 MB QSPI log read back over USB CDC. For the bench window where "the rails are up but USB can't be trusted yet", use **SEGGER RTT**: a RAM ring buffer that the debugger drains over SWD without halting the core, unlike semihosting, which would stall the 800 Hz ISR. Zero pins, zero parts. *(Unverified: RTT reliability with openocd + ST-LINK on the H7 — verify before the boards arrive, not during bring-up.)*

**The SWD header must be fitted, not DNP.** A 1×6 straight header weighs under a gram, is held by six through-hole joints in 1.6 mm board, and carries no cable in flight — it is not this board's vibration risk (that is the two 8 × 10 mm capacitors). Making it DNP means hand-soldering a header onto a board that just came back from a crash before you can talk to it — the worst possible time to add new solder joints.

**Rules for synchronous trace (procedural, with a physical backstop)**: PE3–PE6 are ETM synchronous trace TRACED0–3. On the STM32H7 trace-pin muxing is ordinary GPIO AF configuration (not forced by `DBGMCU_CR` as on the F1/F4), so the hazard is lower than first judged, but a probe with AHB-AP access can still write GPIOE MODER/AFR itself. Two rules: **(i) trace permanently set to async or off in every IDE / openocd configuration, never synchronous 1/2/4-bit; (ii) whenever anything is connected to J2, the arming plug must be out — that is the physical guarantee; the IDE setting is only a belt.** One collateral effect nobody noticed: PE2 is both TRACECK and `QSPI_IO2`, so whoever enables synchronous trace also breaks the flight-log interface.

> **Recommendation: do not move the pyro gates off PE4/PE5**, even though there were ~41 free GPIOs and no routing yet. Reason: the mechanism has been downgraded to "procedurally controllable", while choosing two new pins must simultaneously avoid AN2606 Table 113, AF0 pins with reset pull-ups, TT-class pins and the §3.10 reserved table — **re-deriving a frozen choice and getting it wrong is exactly this project's most frequently observed error class**. The cost, stated plainly: a procedural rule that people must follow.

## 5. Changes that make errors recoverable

**This list was deliberately cut short.** Every cuttable link and every DNP position is a new failure path, and "building hardware around silence in the documentation" is a mistake this project has already made. **Three kept, seven rejected.**

### Kept

| # | Location | Which design error it rescues | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | **`U5.6` (MS5611 SDO) changed from a copper short to a 10k pull-down to GND** (one 0402 from the existing BOM line `C25744`) | SDO is type **O (output)** in the datasheet's pin configuration table. In I2C mode it is unused (the PS mode table says so), but "unused" does not mean "high-impedance". ### Explicitly rejected (written down so you know the list was trimmed)

- **A cuttable link from `U4.6` (ICM-45686 pin 6) to GND — rejected.** The ICM-42688-P pin table (the land-compatibility target named in this design) says **pin 6 = GND, "Power supply ground"**. The schematic is right and **the frozen document is wrong** (it names 13 of 14 pins, omitting exactly the power ground). Putting a scalpel link into the IMU's power-ground return creates a new failure path for a hazard that does not exist. **Fix the document, not the board.**
- **A common cuttable link for `U4`'s reserved pins (2/3/7/10/11) — rejected.** The datasheet explicitly allows 2/3/10/11 to be GND or NC and **requires** pin 7 to be GND. No configuration exists in which floating them would be better.
- **A series cuttable link on L2 — rejected.** Cut, it removes power from every sensor (GPS included), and it puts a scored 0.25 mm of copper in series with three sensors' supply at 30 g. R2's parallel DNP position gives all the useful freedom without touching the series path.
- **A snap-off sensor coupon — rejected.** It comes out of the same box on the same day as the main board, so it cannot rescue "discovered after assembly". It costs the board's scarcest line (ICM-45686, 5,496 pcs and falling) + 25 % of board area + a snap-off, whose flexing stress lands right next to the sensor cluster — board flex cracking MLCCs is one of the best-documented latent defects in electronics manufacturing, and this board has nine 0805 X5Rs. **The right fix is registering with TDK to download DS-000577**, a two-minute form, not a physical constraint.
- **A resistor-divider hardware version code — rejected** (use the 96-bit UID, see §4.4).
- **Series DNP positions on the servo signals — rejected** (`R24`–`R27` already are the cut points).
- **A DNP position for MS5611 CSB address selection — rejected** (firmware probes both 0x76 and 0x77 at zero cost, which also closes the datasheet's own ambiguity).
- **QSPI series-resistor / WP-HOLD pull-up positions — rejected** (the frozen document already showed 3× margin: 140 ps one way over 20 mm against ≥ 1 ns edges).
- **Bringing the 0 Ω current-sense jumper back — rejected.** Deleting it was right, but the document's reason was only half right: D2 does provide "lift the branch to find a short", but **a Schottky's forward drop is logarithmic in current, ± 30 % spread and strongly temperature-dependent, meaningless as a shunt below 100 mA**. The better reason: two more solder joints in series with the MCU's only supply path adds links to a single-point failure chain. **Designate five existing isolation points** and print them on the assembly drawing: `D2` (RAW → logic), `D3` (USB → logic), `L1` (buck → 3V3), `L2` (3V3 → 3V3A), the arming plug (RAW → PYRO_BUS). Measure current with the bench supply's own ammeter at `TP-VLOGIC` / `TP-3V3`. The pyro branch needs no shunt: the AO3400A's own `Rds(on)` is one (about 170 mV at 8 A, ± 30 %, enough to tell 5 A from 0.5 A).
- **Pull-pin moved to an ADC + pin change — pin change rejected; compensate in firmware.** The structurally correct fix is a 4.7k inside the removable plug with the node read on an ADC, so "short / pin in / pin pulled or wire broken" become three distinguishable bands. But that requires moving `PULLPIN` from PD14 to a pin that is ADC-capable, FT-class (the wire leaves the board) and not in the §3.10 reserved table — that most frequent error class again. **Use a pure-firmware transition latch instead**: maintain `pin_was_seen_installed`, set only after PD14 has read low (0.30 V) continuously for 500 ms; the READY gate requires `pin_was_seen_installed == true` and PD14 currently high. A wire broken from boot **never reads low**, the latch never sets, and the board reports a specific fault code instead of READY. **Honest residual**: a wire that breaks after boot but before the pin is pulled is still indistinguishable from a pull — the same property as any pull-pin interlock. Operating cost: the pin must be physically inserted **before** every power-up, or the board never reaches READY — exactly the discipline you want.

### 6.2 Worth doing (by value for money)

1. **Static-port specification (free, and the prerequisite for the barometer as primary apogee source).** Zero words in the frozen document. Specification: **3 or 4 holes, evenly spaced around the coupler wall** (not 1 — the airframe rolls, so a single hole's reading is roll-dependent), at the board's y = 0–30 mm section (battery end), **≥ 60 mm from the SAM-M10Q** (u-blox UBX-22020019 §4.4: the TCXO is sensitive to sudden ambient temperature changes; "avoid rapid temperature changes and vents near the module"), **never on a bulkhead facing the deployment compartment**. Total area about one Ø1.6 mm hole per 100 cm³ of bay volume (*hobby rule of thumb, unverified; re-check against the actual bay volume*), deburred inside. Too small → pressure lag → late apogee → drogue at high speed; too large → noise and roll sensitivity. Print an arrow `VENT THIS END` on the board. **Cost: one drill bit.**
2. **Fill bulkhead wire pass-throughs on both sides with neutral-cure RTV** (after the pyro and servo harnesses are through). **The check must tape over the static ports before blowing in air** — the bay is vented by design, so without taping the ports the air escapes through them and the test **always passes**. Cost: one tube, 10 minutes; re-routing means digging out and resealing.
3. **Bootlace ferrules + torque-stripe paint.** Bare stranded copper cold-flows under a screw clamp — the root cause of "measured fine on the bench, open on the pad". After tightening, draw a contrasting paint stripe across each screw head and terminal body; a broken stripe means the screw turned — turning a mechanical property that cannot be measured in the field into a three-second visual check (photograph it the first time as the assembly-drawing reference). Add "all 10 stripes intact" to the checklist. **Cost: about $25 of crimper + about $10 of paint, once; 5 minutes per re-termination.**
4. **A Ø3.2 mm zip-tie hole on each side of J5** (straddling the header array, 9.0 mm each side of its centre, ≥ 2.0 mm from any copper or plated hole). A 2.5 mm tie comes up through one hole, **presses on the four plug housings** (not on the wires), and goes back down through the other. JR/Futaba plugs are held only by friction with no latch, and they share a board corner with the battery terminal. The assembly drawing states: "the tie must press on the plug bodies; tying only the wires is a defect." Also tie the harness **to the sled** 30 mm downstream so harness loads don't reach the board. **Cost: two drilled holes inside existing margins; cut and re-tie whenever a servo is unplugged.**
5. **Proper mechanical restraint for the battery.** A 40–80 g battery at 92–367 g is 3.7–29 kgf; hook-and-loop is enough at the low end, not the high end. Zip ties through two sled holes over the battery + a compressed pad of closed-cell foam. **And leave room for swelling** — not a tight slot, or a gassing pouch pushes on the board or jams a bulkhead, turning a battery-ageing event into a deployment failure.
6. **Write the MS5611 temperature into every 1 Hz record + a slow "hot" tone above 55 °C.** The temperature word is already computed for pressure compensation, so it is free. **No temperature gate that refuses arming** — a 60 °C gate would false-reject in the review's own nominal environment (60–75 °C bay air), and the MS5611's absolute temperature accuracy is ± 2.0 °C (−20..85 °C) and it reads its own die temperature on 3V3A, not free bay air; after the first false rejection the operator comments out that line, **taking the other eight checks in the same AND gate with it**. If a gate is unavoidable, set it at **80 °C**, above every nominal value, so that tripping it means something really is wrong.
7. **Preload the two 470 µF cans with closed-cell foam** (from can top to sled/bay cover), no adhesive. See the rejection reasons below. *(Superseded by MARGIN_PRESCRIPTION.md §4.13: stake them.)*
8. **Functional tap/shake test (60 s, replacing the impossible shaker table).** Powered on the assembled sled running flight firmware, logging at 500 Hz: tap every tall part (both cans, both inductors, the buzzer, the four through-hole connectors, the GPS module) and the board edges with a plastic screwdriver handle, and drop it 200 mm onto a wooden bench 20 times on each of its six faces. Pass: no resets in the log, no IMU DRDY gaps, no `PYRO1/2_CONT` transitions, no `ARM_SENSE` flicker, no `VBAT_SENSE` glitches. **Ten minutes, zero parts, zero analysis code, and it finds intermittent joints, loose plugs and marginal screw clamps — the three failures that actually kill amateur flight computers.**
9. **Heat-soak test: not in a kitchen oven.** A kitchen oven's lowest stable setting is often 80–90 °C, **above the buzzer's own 70 °C limit**, and would damage the unit under test. Use **the dashboard of a closed car in the sun** — exactly the environment in item (f) of the brief. Put the sealed bay in for an hour, let the board log its own MS5611 temperature, then re-run the signature table and every self-test.
10. **Pull-pin harness < 150 mm, twisted pair, physically separate from the servo/pyro harness** (other side, different ties). This is the ruling on open item 6 in frozen §8; implementing it means a second SRV05-4 is **not** needed. If they must be bundled, add +1 part.
11. **15-minute post-landing routine**: visual check for residue (if present, **dry brush, never an IPA wash**) → the 10 paint stripes → re-run the tap test and compare against the noise floor / `VBAT` droop baseline recorded at build (a cracking joint shows up first as a changed noise floor, months before it opens in flight) → MS5611 PROM CRC-4 + comparison against the known site elevation (CRC cannot catch a blocked or contaminated opening) → download the log and check `RCC_RSR`, the boot counter, SUSPECT flags and MON-RF anomalies.

### 6.3 Explicitly not done (each with its reason)

- **Whole-board conformal coating — not done.** Four reasons, any one sufficient: **(a)** the TE datasheet says `Cleaning might damage the sensor!` and requires no-clean paste — so this board **can never be washed** and will always carry no-clean flux residue plus hand-soldering residue; **coating over ionic residue is worse than not coating**, sealing the contaminant onto the copper together with moisture that permeates acrylic — a textbook reliability defect that can create the very leakage path you meant to prevent. **(b)** The mask-off list is nearly the whole board: the MS5611's stainless cap and two 0.5 mm vent holes, the MLT-8530 sound port (coated = mute), the SAM-M10Q patch antenna and its 10 mm border (coating shifts the ceramic resonance), the USB-C cavity, the J1/J4 screw clamps and wire entries, the J2/J5/J6 pin fields, every test point and both LED lenses — what is left after masking is not worth the step. **(c)** If potting is done, **silicone and acrylic are chemically incompatible**: low-surface-energy siloxanes migrate across the board, and the acrylic then craters, fish-eyes and delaminates — including in areas the silicone never touched. **(d)** The main risk it guards against is already bought out by the 1k pyro pull-down (recomputed with the corrected `Vgs(th)` min = 0.65 V, reaching threshold takes a surface-insulation failure of about **12 kΩ**; real SIR failures are in the megohm range). **Alternative**: seal the bulkhead pass-throughs (the main air path) + dry brushing after landing. If coating is ever required, use a solder-through single-part acrylic (MG 419D / HumiSeal 1B31), brush it locally only on the few high-impedance nodes, and accept the mask-off list above. *(Superseded by MARGIN_PRESCRIPTION.md §4.13, which makes local coating of the pyro block and SWD header mandatory after the gate-network change.)*
- **Mid-span support / potting the board to the sled — not done.** See the fatigue scaling in §6.1: about 5× real margin (3× even over a 20-flight life). The previous version rated this VEHICLE_LOSS and proposed the most expensive mechanical change in the document by **judging a 600-cycle exposure against a 2 × 10⁷-cycle criterion**; the same document says "a 1–4 s burn makes classical high-cycle fatigue essentially irrelevant". All the saved mechanical budget goes to items 1–5 of §6.2. If a mid-span support is wanted for another reason (handling stiffness, keeping the 14 mm LQFP100 from flexing on a hard landing), **argue it from shock, not fatigue** — a single 92–367 g overload, judged on lead yield, not S-N life.
- **RTV on the two 470 µF cans — not done; foam preload instead.** Acetoxy RTV releases acetic acid that corrodes silver terminations and bare copper; any adhesive turns rework of a cheap capacitor into scrapping the board; a fillet on one side only adds tip mass asymmetrically and may push the resonance somewhere worse; and adhesive traps the heat of a 25 mΩ ESR, 4.1 Arms polymer can (already the shortest-lived part on the board). And **vibration fatigue is not this board's problem anyway** (previous item); the thing to guard against is landing shock — foam preload does that more directly, with no chemistry, and stays reworkable. *(Superseded by MARGIN_PRESCRIPTION.md §4.13, which found a 1–3 kHz cantilever resonance inside the vibration band and requires staking.)*
- **An impossible modal verification ("tap and confirm the ringing moved above 500 Hz") — deleted.** At 800 Hz ODR the Nyquist limit is 400 Hz; modes above 500 Hz **cannot appear in the data at all**, and the ICM-45686's anti-alias filter removes them before the ADC anyway. As written, the test can never return the answer it looks for, and it would require writing an FFT and ringing fit. Use the functional tap test in §6.2 item 8 instead.
- **Panelising for Standard PCBA to buy SPI inspection — not done.** Three reasons: SPI is in-line process control after stencil printing and before placement; it gates the line and **produces no per-board solder-joint report you can hold**, and reflow, warpage and coplanarity all happen after it; panelising forces an even board count (need 5, buy 6) and charges assembly per board; and snapping a panel is the textbook cause of cracked 0805 X5Rs — this board has nine 22 µF 0805s, and one cracked on 3V3 is an intermittent fault that appears only under vibration and never on the bench. **The "invisible LGA-14 joint" worry is already solved for free**: the six-face 16384 LSB/g check exercises all 14 joints on every board and says more than SPI would. Spend the same $25–35 on JLC through-hole soldering, which nothing else covers. (Also: a single 50 × 110 mm board is already inside Economic's 10 × 10 – 470 × 500 range, so **no panel means no snap-off step**.)
- **Consigned AO3400A / AON6403 — not done.** `C20917` is a JLC Basic part at $0.0677; consignment means buying from Mouser, shipping to Hong Kong, paying about $30 handling (*unverified*), accepting lead-time loss and taking on receiving-mismatch risk — to control about $0.20 of parts on 5 boards. **The handling fee is 150× the part price, and a safeguard nobody will actually follow is worth zero.** The C number itself is the brand choice (relabels are different SKUs with different brand fields); with the silkscreen C-number table + a PCBA order note + the 20-cycle dry-fire test, the residual risk is quantified. *(Unverified: JLCPCB has no public "do not substitute" flag; the `Shortage Preference` documentation says explicitly that it governs the number of boards shipped, not part substitution. Open a ticket before ordering and keep the reply.)*
- **Measuring the Miller kick on an oscilloscope as an acceptance gate — not done.** Corrected Crss = 50 pF (75 pF is Coss) and `Vgs(th)` min = 0.65 V give **6.8×** margin. Two test points + an oscilloscope session + ten minutes per board to measure a single transient with 6.8× margin — on a mechanically bouncing plug contact, millimetres from a radiating 8.4 V step — is terrible value, and a test a hobbyist with one scope does once and never again. **Test the outcome instead**: fit a 1 Ω dummy load + an LED through 1 kΩ, have firmware drive PE4 low, **cycle the arming plug 20 times; pass = zero flashes in 20**. That tests the assembled circuit as it really is (real Crss, real Ciss, the 10 nF really fitted and really returned to the source) rather than one parameter. Keep the gate test points — the bottom is empty and two 1.5 mm pads are free — but do not use a scope reading as an acceptance gate.
- **A 2 Ω / 50 W resistor `Rds(on)` rig — not done.** 4.2 A into 2 Ω is **35 W**; a bare wirewound resistor without a heatsink runs at 200–300 °C — an ignition source on a bench that also holds pyrotechnics, and the previous version priced it as "$2 of ground equipment" without a word about heat or mounting. Measure under loads you apply anyway: four servos stalled together, or a 20 ms fire pulse into a 1 Ω dummy load (about 8 A: a genuine part reads 8 × 2 × 3 mΩ = **48 mV**, a 20 mΩ relabel **320 mV**). For a dedicated load use two 12 V 21 W car bulbs in parallel — self-limiting, $2, cannot start a fire.
- **Incoming `Vgs(th)` screening of loose parts — do it once, don't make it a regime.** It can only screen your own rework spares, not the parts JLC actually placed. $0.50 (33k resistor + 9 V battery). Use the **corrected** limits: AO3400A `Vgs(th)` = **0.65 / 1.05 / 1.45 V** (not 0.4–1.1; the previous limits would reject typical genuine parts as fakes, and a screen that rejects good parts is worse than none — after the first false rejection the whole discipline is gone); AON6403 `|Vgs(th)|` = **1.2 / 1.7 / 2.2 V** (not 1.4–2.2). The half that really discriminates is **a spread > 0.2 V across 5 parts from the same tape = relabel**. *Two of the previous round's three numeric criteria turned out wrong against the primary datasheets — so treat every remaining numeric band in this document (the USBLC6's 6.0–6.8 V clamp window, the 0.6–0.75 V diode-drop window) as unverified, and have someone actually read the original documents before release to fab.*
- **Nylon mounting hardware (removing metal from near the GPS antenna) — not adopted by this plan.** It belongs to the RF domain, and it directly weakens two of the four mounting points on a board whose dominant mechanical load is landing shock. **Answer the question by measurement instead**: enable `UBX-MON-RF` at 1 Hz and log `jamInd` / `agcCnt` / `noisePerMS` plus a per-satellite C/N₀ histogram in every bench and flight record; on the bench compare the buck running under load against the buck pushed from PFM into CCM with a 10 Ω dummy load, comparing mean C/N₀ and jamInd. **One correction along the way**: the frozen document's "77.4 dB @ 1.1 MHz" is a number at 1.1 MHz and says nothing about 1575.42 MHz — 1575.42 / 1.1 = 1432.2, and accounting for the AP63203's … *(the original text ends here, truncated)*
