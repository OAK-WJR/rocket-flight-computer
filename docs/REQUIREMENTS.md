# Flight computer requirements (first principles)

What the board must do, derived from the project goals (`../../CLAUDE.md`), the owner's decisions,
the flight envelope of both flights, and the board's own design points. Every design review checks
the board against this file. Verification column: **CAD** = KiCad files and checks, **Calc** =
datasheet calculation, **Bench** = measured on a built board, **Flight**.

## Sources

- **Project goals:**
  - hold roll rate near zero, and fly a commanded roll program (e.g. a 90° turn and hold);
  - flight 1 on an AeroTech K850DM, about Mach 1.45, roll only (owner confirmation; parent CLAUDE.md);
  - flight 2 on an L1000W, about Mach 1.9, possibly adding pitch/yaw;
  - single deploy at apogee: one primary pyro channel, with a fully isolated Featherweight Blue Jay backup.
- **Owner decisions:**
  - one board flies both flights with no respin, so flight 2's worst case applies now;
  - flight condition (Mach, dynamic pressure) is estimated from accelerometer + barometer;
  - roll angle is integrated gyro, relative to the orientation at liftoff;
  - on a flight-computer reset, hang or brownout the tab must return to neutral quickly;
  - roll control is active during the burn as well as the coast (exact start and stop points open);
  - flight 2's pitch/yaw surfaces are undecided, so the board's 4-servo limit becomes their constraint;
  - no in-flight tab-deflection sensing (servos are characterised on the bench, under load);
  - the telemetry radio and GPS are on the board (decision 2026-10-15, replacing R6's external radio and GPS modules): GPS u-blox MAX-F10S, radio EBYTE E22-900MM22S, both JLCPCB-assembled (F10; selection in `../../reports/Compact rocket GPS and radio.md`); the radio uplink must never arm or fire recovery;
  - Tripoli/NAR safety code.
- **Flight envelope:** the parent project and owner confirmation supersede the old I500T baseline.

  | | Flight 1 (K850DM) | Flight 2 (L1000W) |
  |---|---|---|
  | Motor / booster | 54 × 635 mm / 721 mm | 54 × 635 mm / 721 mm |
  | Max Mach, project design point | About 1.45 | About 1.9 |
  | Vehicle mass, axial acceleration, altitude and duration | Recalculate for K850DM; do not reuse the I500T values | Earlier L1000W estimate: 3.7–4.9 kg, 28–37 g, 3.4–3.6 km, 460–525 s; update for the current vehicle and Mach 1.9 design point |

  These are project design points and inherited estimates, not flight measurements.
  K850DM certification lists 2041 g motor mass and 1224 g propellant; the old vehicle
  2.36/2.05 kg mass pair is not applicable. Sources and disposition: M3_R6.md.
  Keep ≥ ±64 g accelerometer capability (U4 supports higher ranges), ±4000 dps gyro,
  a ≤30 s high-rate control window, ≥10 km barometer capability and the 100 g
  retention design load. Final vehicle simulation/weighing must establish that the
  selected sensor ranges, logging duration, servo/harness loads and battery reserve
  cover both flights before launch.

  The earlier M0.9 stuck-tab roll estimate was about 1100 °/s; the earlier uncontrolled
  roll estimate was 1000–1850 °/s. They are historical estimates, not K850DM predictions.
  Scaling the latter linearly to M1.9 approaches ±4000 dps (Inference); verify range
  and control margin against the updated flight model.

| F6 | Failsafe. **Hardware:** servo signals are a defined low through reset, brownout and boot; the logic supply rides through power-switch bounce of ≥ 0.15 s from the 7.4 V launch floor up (worst case 0.21 s at 7.4 V; 0.11 s at 6.0 V; about 0.13 s if four servos are stalled at the 7.4 V floor at the same moment, Inference), and the dips of a four-servo stall from a pack inside the operating assumptions below; pyro gates are off in reset. **A brownout longer than the ride-through** restarts the board in up to about 1.1 s (C87 recharges through 227 kΩ); meanwhile the servo is powered without pulses, then unpowered. **Tab to neutral quickly** after a reset or hang (target ≤ 50 ms from fault to neutral command, proposed here): neutral is the first servo output after any reset, and a stalled roll loop is caught and the tab commanded neutral within the target without waiting for a reset. No single sensor, bus or loop failure may cause repeated resets or block deployment, and the watchdog is serviced in every phase from the pad to landing (FMEA V-1: a watchdog started in hardware and fed by the IMU-driven loop resets forever if the IMU never comes up). Allocation that meets both: FMEA F-1's structure (a 100 Hz safety task, independent of the IMU, supervises the roll loop and alone feeds the hardware watchdog at the end of each complete pass) with the IWDG period cut from ~400 ms to ≤ 32 ms; the roll-control interrupt bounds its own run time (a timeout on every sensor transfer), so only a fault can starve the safety task, and the watchdog then resets the board; reset release takes up to about 6 ms after a watchdog reset (C24, 100 nF on NRST, charging through the MCU's 30–50 kΩ pull-up; MARGIN_PRESCRIPTION P1-6), and neutral (1520 µs) follows within about 10 ms of release (clock and timer start-up, Inference). Any hang, in the roll interrupt, the safety task or the CPU, then ends in a neutral command within about 48 ms, while a dead IMU stops the roll interrupt instead of resetting the board. Work that cannot fit a ≤ 32 ms pass (flash erase) runs before flight or in bounded slices. The board supports this as built (STM32H743 IWDG from the 32 kHz LSI, servo lines a defined low through reset); it is a firmware requirement, verified on the bench. The HV6120's behaviour with no pulses, unpowered under hinge load, and with its input against the board's 11 kΩ reset pull-down is measured on the bench. A break in the battery lead itself is ridden through for only about 5–15 ms (C84; about 3–6 ms while the on-board radio transmits, `sources/r7_review/pass1_electrical.md` §4.4; about 1–3 ms with the camera on, since C84 then also feeds it through U11, Inference): the battery harness has crimped or soldered leads and a locking fuse holder. **Pyro after a reset in flight:** re-enabled only under FMEA §7.4′.4 (live post-lift-off evidence), never by default | CAD, Calc, Bench |

| F8 | Tripoli/NAR. Pyro is disarmed until the ARM plug goes in on the pad; the ARM plug is a complete break of the CH1 pyro bus; nothing fires at power-up or reset, on ARM-link insertion or removal (contact bounce included), or while armed with the MCU unpowered, in reset, booting or idle (bench gates with dummy loads before any real igniter, ORDERING §5). Power is switched by an external reed/magnetic or dry-circuit switch. Pyro terminals cannot be mistaken for the battery or camera terminals: black 2-pin ARM (J124) and IGNITER 1 (J121) screw terminals (the one stocked body) told apart by their ARM and IGN legends, green 4-pin battery/switch terminal J1, and a keyed 4-pin GH camera connector, with unambiguous pin legends. Pyro copper keeps its spacing on every layer, enforced by DRC (`m3.kicad_dru`, nine rules): RAW_ACT ≥ 1.0 mm from bus and igniter copper (the ARM break), ≥ 0.5 mm from every gate and sense net and ≥ 0.3 mm from RAW_ACT_SENSE; bus/igniter/drain ≥ 0.5 mm from gate nets, all GND copper and every other live net, and ≥ 0.3 mm from sense nets; gate nets ≥ 0.3 mm from every other live net inside the pyro block; and, against a single solder bridge, every exposed bus, igniter, drain and RAW_ACT pad ≥ 1.0 mm from every sense and gate pad and every divider-midpoint pad ≥ 1.0 mm from every gate pad, the actuator switch's gate nets included (rule H). Exposed gate copper near bus or 3V3 copper is covered by the mandatory local coating (P3). No single shorted or open resistor in a sense divider may drive an MCU pin past its ratings or pass meaningful current through an igniter: each divider's top is two 5.1 kΩ in series and its bottom two 2 kΩ in parallel, so any one fault leaves the node at ≤ 1.4 V (3.3 V at the 19.9 V clamp) and an armed igniter at ≤ 1.4 mA; each ADC pin also has its own 4.7 kΩ (R168, R170 and R172 (the surviving CH1 and RAW_ACT ADC paths)), which limits the current if two faults coincide (both halves of a top shorted: about 1 mA into the pin, Inference, igniter ≤ about 9.4 mA). ## Accepted risks (recorded decisions)

- **No on-board servo-fault containment.** A hard short on a servo lead or inside a servo draws tens of amps from the pack until the external 15 A fuse opens. The primary computer goes down with it, and recovery relies on the isolated backup altimeter (R2_VERIFICATION §2). On-board fusing of four servo feeds does not fit the 2.54 mm header's middle row. If containment is wanted, put an inline fast fuse on each servo's + lead in the harness.
- **The tab's state while the computer reboots depends on the servo** (hold or go limp with no pulses): bench item under F6. A brownout longer than the ride-through extends the reboot to about 1.1 s.
- **Flight 2's servo harness** runs in an external raceway at up to M1.75, so a lead short is more likely: per-servo inline fuses are recommended there.
- **A reversed 3S pack** (two faults at once) can ring U9's input to about −19 V, past its −15 V limit.
- **Harness miswiring remains hazardous.** Camera J9 is now keyed GH, eliminating the former green screw-terminal ambiguity. An incorrectly assembled adapter can still put a recovery load on a powered harness; continuity-check connector polarity and wiring with all recovery loads disconnected.
- **Gate-net spacing outside the pyro block** is the board's 0.15 mm default (under mask or inner layers, covered by JLC's electrical test; a bridge to an always-on net shows in the plug-out cold test). Inside the block DRC keeps 0.3 mm.
- **No hardware revision ID**: firmware cannot tell this board from another revision by itself; the firmware build is bound to the board by hash (AGENTS.md §3).
