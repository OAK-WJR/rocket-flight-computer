# M3-ASSY-R9: clean-sheet B-spine layout

R9 is R8's circuit (the same parts, LCSC codes and U1 pin map) laid out again from a clean sheet: nothing is copied
rigidly from R7 or R8, every safety and RF qualification is re-derived on the new geometry, and a rule-based gate
(`tools/check_r9.py`) replaces R8's hash-asserted copper. The owner's priorities: correctness and every requirement
first, then simple routing and layout that make the design robust. The plan is
`m3_design/assembly_review/sources/r9_design/r9_plan.md` (B-spine), the owner's answers `R9_OWNER_DECISIONS.md`
(2026-10-20), and every agent decision `R9_DECISIONS.md` (D-S1-01 … D-S10-19, and review pass 1's fix pass D-F1-01 …
D-F1-11), each with its basis.

- **Board:** `m3_design/assembly_review/m3.kicad_pcb`, SHA-256
  `4f841a0e44ebb1829ad7996dfe6320ed527fce1fdb1e5ff11658e0705b44ac14` (review pass 1's fix pass; step 10's board was
  `fc3d5b34…`), **46 × 102 mm**, 4 layers (JLC041611-7628), parts on both faces. Netlist `schematic.net.xml` `8b0e04ea565ecf1b…`; design rules `m3.kicad_dru` `ea77b791eaa4026d…`.
- **Build:** `m3_design/r9_build.sh` rebuilds it byte for byte from R8's committed sources (9c8179b), stages 0–38:
  schematic and footprint variants, placement (`r9_place.tsv`), the RF strip, the pyro column, power, ten routing
  families, ground, silkscreen, each stage followed by its gate, then `tools/check.sh`. No autorouter and no hand-patched
  copper. Fab package: `m3_design/r9_fab.sh`.
- **Branch:** `m3-assy-r9` (worktree `rocket-flight-computer-r9`), committed and pushed at the accepted state on

## What changed from R8

- **Clean sheet.** R8 held the RF strip, the CH1 block, J1, J122, the holes and the outline rigid by hash
  (`check_r8`, 12 digest groups). R9 holds none of them: the RF chains and launches are regenerated by template in each
  jack's and module's frame and shown congruent with R7's qualified geometry (D-S4-01), the pyro column is drawn from its
  pads (D-S5-07), and `check_r9` judges every block by rules (P-0 … P-11, R-1 … R-13, M-2 … M-7, F9-1, T-1, the K-rules;
  D-S1-01 … 25).
- **Length 102 mm** (R8 118), owner decision 1's last fallback (D-S3-03). The plan's 98 mm and the 100 mm fallback
  cannot legalise the four MCU-end interface resistors (R306 / R310 / R313 / R315, RF parts by F10's list) against
  r9_plan §2's hard row X9 (RF parts ≥ 5.94 mm from U1 / U3 / Y1, R8's measured R306–U1 distance) and the SERIES rule.
  Under F10's own 5 mm the kill path holds at 100 mm (total lane detour 6.96 mm, against 2.62 mm at 102 mm; 98 mm
  still fails; R9P1-REQUIREMENTS-01, `m3_design/r9_kill_path.py` with the part separation at 5.0 mm); only the four
  resistor spots are shown, the rest of a 100 mm board is Inference. Both options are the owner's (R9_VALIDATION C1).
- **Layout (B-spine, r9_plan §1):** the RF strip aft (y 0–22.0, owner decision 3); U1 at rotation 0 in the middle with
  the flash and crystal under it; the CH1 pyro column on the west edge as a dead end, J124 (ARM) forward of J121
  (IGNITER 1), both wires entering from −x (owner decision 1); all power in the east column: J1.1 → the facing FETs →
  a RAW_ACT spine on F.Cu, B.Cu and In2 → C121, J124.2 and J122's RAW row; J122 turned (owner decision 5 default).
- **Footprint variants (owner decision 2):** J121 and J124 use `M3_DB128L_2P_BK_SW` and J122
  `HDR-TH_12P-P2.54-V-M-R3-C4-S2.54_MIR`: the same part on the same land with the pad numbering mirrored, so each pad
