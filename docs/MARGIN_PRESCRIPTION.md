# Rocket Flight Computer v1 — Margin Prescription (an executable translation of the "10×" requirement)

> **Operating-point convention**: steady-state items use an av-bay ambient of **TA = 70 °C**; 20 ms pyro-pulse items use **TA = 60 °C** (the original derivation point; if the 70 °C design ambient is adopted, add 10 K to every pyro Tj). 70 °C itself is an **assumption** — its +30 K solar term comes from data on horizontal closed enclosures; an upright airframe in wind is more likely +15…+20 K. **Measure it with a temperature logger at a hot range day**; do not use it to manufacture a "nine parts over temperature" conclusion.
> **Two upstream conflicts run through this whole document; do not release a board until they are resolved**: see §8.1 (outline/airframe) and §8.2 (servo current baseline, 7.48 A vs 12 A).

---

## 1. Direct answer

**No. This board cannot have 10× everywhere, and chasing 10× on the power path is the wrong engineering target.**

"10×" means completely different things for different quantities, so it must be answered by category. **In the time/information domain** (CPU load, SPI utilisation, timer resolution, watchdog false trips, continuity-test current) 10× is free and the board is already at 20×–2600×; nothing to buy. **For log capacity, Miller immunity, flash burst absorption and apogee thresholds** 10× is also essentially free — only firmware constants change: they are currently 0.97×, 3.8×, 0.67× and 7.6×, and become 10.5×, 13×, 341× and 13.2× — these must be changed. **For continuous current in power semiconductors, connector contact current, bus copper, logic brown-out hold-up and gyro full scale**, 10× is **physically impossible** at this package scale: replacing the AO3400A with the best part that fits, a DFN3×3 (AON7524), gives at most 10.8× on the applicable pulse rating, and no package that fits reaches 10× on continuous rating (SOT-23 tops out around 6 A, DFN3×3 is 25 A, 10× needs 85 A); the AON6403 load switch's package limit is 17 A @ 70 °C (10× needs 8 in parallel per side, 480 mm² of board, which does not fit); the servo header contact has a physical ceiling of about 5–6 A, so 10× would need a servo stalling at 0.187 A (which does not exist); 10× brown-out hold-up needs 3777 µF (today 470 µF, i.e. eight Ø8 × 10 mm cans); ±4000 dps is already the ICM-45686's top range and the ceiling of consumer MEMS, giving only **2.00×** against a 2000 dps control-surface runaway, and no part can improve that. **Voltage/temperature ratios** (junction temperature, Vgs, ambient) are not quantities "10×" can express at all — junction temperature is an absolute scale where 10× is undefined; the correct target is "at least 50 K below Tj(max)".

The honest conclusion: **spend the entire 10× budget on the inhibit path (do not fire when you shouldn't) and the information path, stop the power path at 1.3×–2.2×, and cover the rest with redundancy and fail-safes.** That is also the direction safety logic demands — a backup commercial altimeter covers every "should have fired but didn't" risk, but it **only adds** to the "fired when it shouldn't have" surface, so the latter has to be solved on the board itself.

## 2. Industry standards reference (read this first — it calibrates everything after it)

| Standard | Class | Derating factor | Equivalent margin |
|---|---|---|---|

| **NASA GSFC-STD-1000 Rev I** Table 3.07-1 (flight software) | CPU mean 30 % / deadline 10 % / writable NVM 30 % / data interfaces 10 % | **1.43× / 1.11× / 1.43× / 1.11×** — ⚠ one re-check failed to retrieve the table itself, marked **unverified** |

**Say this to yourself plainly**: the strictest public derating standard for spaceflight hardware asks for only **1.33×** on MOSFET current and 1.67× on power. A literal 10× current margin means **100× power margin**, **7.5 times** stricter than NASA asks of a spacecraft. It cannot be done on a 40 mm (or 50 mm) board at any price. Wherever this document says "10× is impossible", that is not laziness — it is past the edge of physics.

The limits of the standard must also be stated honestly: EEE-INST-002 was written for spacecraft that spend ten unrepairable years in vacuum. Its §C1 Table 4 Note 2 requires "ceramic capacitors rated ≥ 100 Vdc in < 10 Vdc applications" — **no part on this board** can meet that (there is no 100 V 22 µF in 0805) — and its screening / qualification / lot-traceability requirements (most of that document, and the main reason space parts are actually reliable) are impossible to meet on a JLCPCB assembly line. **Derating is necessary, not sufficient** — quoting its factors while dropping its notes is exactly the class of error this review exists to prevent.

## 4. Required changes (sorted by consequence of failure × how marginal it is)

### P0-1 Pyro gate network — the highest-value change on the board, $0.006, a prerequisite for everything else

**Problem**: the netlist's `PYRO1_GATE` node carries R-GATE1 (470R), R-PD1 (1k), the Q-PY1 gate and C-GS1 (10 nF) together. A MOSFET gate draws no DC, so this is a **0.680 DC divider, not a series gate resistor**. Vgs = 2.245 V typ, **1.93 V at the guaranteed corner**, while AO3400A Rev 3.1 **is only characterised down to 2.5 V** (48 mΩ max @ 3 A). A worst-threshold device (Vgs(th) max 1.45 V) gets only 0.48 V of overdrive and may saturate at 8.5 A: several volts dropped, tens of watts dissipated, self-destruction within 20 ms — possibly without lighting the igniter. It is the one single point on the board that can both "fail to fire" and "burn into a drain-source short (= fires the next time the arming plug goes in)".

**Fix (zero new part numbers)**:

| Ref | Now | Change to | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| R-GATE1/2 | 470R 0603 (C23179) | **220R 0805 (reuse C17557)** | Raises the divider ratio; MCU peak 15 mA (inside the 20 mA absolute maximum) |
| R-PD1/2 (1k) | On `PYRO*_GATE` | **Moved to `PYRO*_GATE_N` (MCU side)** | Keeps its entire original function |
| New R-GS-PY1/2 | — | **10k 0402 gate → source (reuse C25744)** | Provides the gate-source DC path |
| C-GS1/2 | 10 nF | **47 nF 0603 X7R** (if keeping AO3400A) / **220 nF** (if switching to AON7524) | Miller immunity 3.8× → **13.2×** |

**Result**: Vgs = 10000 / 10220 × VOH = **3.20 V typ / 2.77 V at the guaranteed corner** — from 0.26 V outside the datasheet spec to 0.27 V inside it, with RDS(on) ≤ 48 mΩ **guaranteed**. The gate RC low-pass 220R × 47 nF = 10.3 µs attenuates 100 ns L·di/dt disturbances 103× (47× today — better). With the MCU Hi-Z (reset/dead), gate-source equivalent is (220 + 1k) ∥ 10k = 1.086 kΩ.

**The cost that must be stated (not computed in the original report)**: contamination immunity gets slightly worse. The leakage resistance from 8.4 V to the gate needed to reach the 0.65 V threshold goes from **11.9 kΩ** today to **12.9 kΩ** — **1.09× worse**. 12 kΩ is entirely achievable on a damp FR-4 surface with black-powder residue, so **the conformal coating in §4.13 is upgraded from "good practice" to "mandatory"**. This is also why the fix is not "just change the gate pull-down to 10k" (with a hypothetical internal MCU pull-up that would give 832 mV, above Vgs(th) min; the document's original reason for choosing 1k was right, and it must be preserved by **moving** the resistor, not by deleting or enlarging it).

**The "no substitutes" argument in design document §4.5 must be corrected at the same time**: AO3400A Rev 3.1's true values are **Vgs(th) 0.65/1.05/1.45 V, Ciss 630 pF, Coss 75 pF, Crss 50 pF**; the document's "0.4–1.1 V / Crss 75 pF / Ciss 750 pF" are all three wrong (the Crss figure was copied from Coss). **But the "no substitutes" rule itself must stay** — the document's two errors nearly cancel (the original 1.01 nC / 750 pF / 0.4 V = 4.3×; the true 1.8 nC / 630 pF / 0.65 V = 3.8×), so the rule accidentally holds; it just has to be re-derived from Rev 3.1, or the next reader will delete the rule because "the argument is wrong".

### P0-2 AO3400A → AON7524 (not optional)

The checker corrected Zth (20 ms) from 6–9 °C/W to **12.5 °C/W** (Fig 11 is normalised to the steady-state RthJA = 125 °C/W; 6–9 would be 0.048–0.072 normalised, contradicting the same datasheet's RthJA (t ≤ 10 s) = 90 and RthJL = 80 max). Result:

| Option | P (20 ms) | ΔTj | Tj @ TA 60 | Against NASA's allowed rise to 110 °C |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| As is (Vgs 2.245 V, Rds unguaranteed, ~74 mΩ est.) | 5.0 W | 62.5 K | 122.5 °C | **0.80×, over** |
| Gate fixed only (AO3400A, Rds 65 mΩ hot at the guaranteed corner) | 4.47 W | 56 K | 116 °C | **0.89×, still over** |
| **AON7524 + gate fix** (Rds ≤ 6.8 mΩ hot, Zth ~6 °C/W) | 0.51 W | 3.1 K | 63 °C | **16.1×** |

**AON7524 (AOS, DFN 3×3 EP, LCSC C431195 — reject the HXY relabel C53262971)**: BVDSS still 30 V (so the document's S2 avalanche-clamp argument is unaffected), RDS(on) **4.4 typ / 5.8 max @ Vgs = 2.5 V** (the **only** DFN3×3 in its class with a guaranteed value at 2.5 V), Vgs(th) 0.4/0.8/1.2 V, IDM 112 A, **EAS 20 mJ + 100 % UIS tested**, VSPIKE 36 V / 100 ns. The EAS rating also closes an internal contradiction the design document created for itself: it added a 1N5819WS freewheel to the buzzer because "the AO3400A datasheet gives no EAS at all", while letting the pyro channels **avalanche unclamped on every flight**.

**Three things that must be done at the same time**: ① C-GS must go to 220 nF (otherwise 5.2 nC of Qgd into 10 nF = 425 mV, **0.94×** against a 0.4 V threshold — **the FET self-turns-on**); ② on DFN3×3 **G (pin 4) and D (pin 5) are adjacent on the same side and not inspectable under the body**; one solder bridge locks the FET on and fires the moment the arming plug goes in — on SOT-23 gate and drain are on opposite sides and the same defect needs a short across the body. That is the **real cost** of this change on the injury axis; the original report calling it "strictly better" was dishonest; ③ therefore **the board-level acceptance test in §4.14 becomes mandatory**.

Area: DFN3×3 lands ~11 mm² vs SOT-23 ~7.5 mm², **+45 % per device, +7 mm² total** (not "no growth"); height 1.3 → 0.8 mm (genuinely lower); the exposed pad is the **drain**, i.e. 3 × 3 mm of copper at PYRO_BUS potential under each device — the layout notes must say so, and ~6 thermal vias are needed.

### P0-3 Fuses — the largest unquantified item on the board, and the only protection fast enough

**(a) The 5 A blade fuse in the arming plug currently has no part number, no manufacturer and no melting I²t.** It is in series with every ampere of every deployment. Two requirements nobody has written down: a legitimate fire of 8.82² × 0.020 = **1.56 A²s** must not blow it (choose **≥ 8 A²s pre-arc I²t**), and an 84 A short-circuit fault must be cleared **within ~2 ms** (otherwise the FET dies shorted = injury mode). **A 5 A fast-acting (F) 5 × 20 mm fuse meets both; the phrase "5 A fuse" also allows a 5 A time-delay (T) part with 5–10× the I²t, which is too slow.** One BOM line + one assembly-procedure line, $0.10–0.30, zero impact on the board.

**(b) More serious: the main path BAT+ → Q1 → common source → Q2 → RAW → four flying servo V+ leads has no fuse, no current limit, no foldback, and firmware cannot intervene.** The design document's own inrush arithmetic gives a **99 A** fault current. Three ordinary faults can reach it: a servo lead chafed through against the airframe wall (thin wire, sharp edge, 30 g), a shorted 470 µF polymer, or a shorted SMBJ12CA (**short is the failure mode of a TVS**, and the document's own §1 argument about "an unfused 25C LiPo" shows the author knew the pack is unprotected). At 99 A the AON6403 pair dissipates ~70 W → fails short → 99 A through a 6.1 mm 1 oz pour → vaporises → **a LiPo fire in a closed airframe**. This is the injury path the whole exercise presupposes, and the five original domain reports did not touch it in a single line.

**Fix**: an **in-line ATO blade fuse holder** in the battery lead. With the MKS HV6120 frozen (7.48 A bus), use a **15 A** element (1.78× against 8.43 A continuous); if the design document's 12 A rule is kept, use **20 A** (only 1.1× against 12 + 5.89 = 17.9 A). **This choice depends directly on the §8.2 ruling.** Zero parts and zero area on the board, ~$2.

### P0-4 One capacitor on GATE_SW — the worst item on the current path, one part

Today τ = 100k × 2 × Ciss (15.2 nF) = 1.52 ms, only **0.80 ms** to the −4.5 V RDS(on) spec point, while a reed/magnetic switch bounces 1–5 ms at 30 g ⚠. The MCU survives one bounce on the 470 µF; a train of bounces longer than the hold-up → MCU reset → §7.4 permanent disarm → **loss of the vehicle, caused by a mechanical contact bouncing**.

**Fix**: add **2.2 µF 0603 X5R (reuse C23630, zero new part numbers)** from GATE_SW to the Q1/Q2 common-source node. Derated at 7.64 V bias, X5R gives about 1.4 µF: τ ≈ 140 ms, ride-through ≈ 175 ms = **93×**, 9.6× the logic hold-up. (A new 1 µF part number gives 124.5 ms / 66×; 0.22 µF only 27 ms, barely 1.5× the hold-up; 4.7 µF gives 585 ms, too much.) Turn-on τ = 10k × 1.4 µF = 14 ms, harmless — the document already showed that inrush is limited by battery internal resistance (99 A / τ = 83 µs), not gate slew.

**A real side benefit (missed in the original report)**: with the capacitor gate-to-source, when an SMBJ12CA clamp event pushes BAT+ to 19.9 V the gate follows capacitively and Vgs stays roughly constant instead of collapsing — the capacitor protects this switch in exactly the transient it is most likely to see.

**The cost that must go into the range procedure**: the board's **only master power switch** moves from "fail safe" toward "fail operational" — the board stays alive 175–350 ms after the magnet is removed. The RSO will ask about this. It also masks a genuinely intermittent switch during bench debugging, so the switch must be tested separately.

### P0-5 Servo series resistor 220R → 1.0 kΩ — the only MCU absolute-maximum violation

There are two chafe-fault cases; the document only computed one:

* **Case A (MCU pin Hi-Z, SRV05-4 clamps to 4.1–4.5 V)**: P = (8.4 − 4.1)² / 220 = 69–84 mW. This is what the document states. 84 mW is 67 % of the 125 mW rating (1.49×), but already **112 % of the NASA-derated 75 mW**, and at 75 °C the allowed power falls to 67 mW → 125 %.
* **Case B (MCU pin actively driving low — it is in this state for 33–67 % of every PWM frame)**: the pin becomes a current sink, P = (8.4 − 0.4)² / 220 = **291 mW = 233 % of rating**, and **36.4 mA flows into the MCU pin = 1.8× the STM32H743's 20 mA IIO absolute maximum**. That is not insufficient derating; it is an absolute-maximum violation.

**Fix**: R-SER1..4 go from 220R 0805 to **1.0 kΩ 0805**. 1.0 kΩ is not arbitrary: it puts the fault current at (8.4 − 0.4) / 1000 = **8.0 mA**, exactly the **specified operating point** at which the STM32 guarantees VOL ≤ 0.4 V, not merely inside the absolute maximum. Case A becomes 18.5 mW (25 % of the derated limit, 4.1×), Case B 64 mW (51 % of rating), and the SRV05-4's sustained fault current drops from 17.7–19.5 mA to 4.3 mA (**4.5× relief**, and that part's continuous forward current was already an admitted unverified item). A 120 ns rise time is irrelevant against a 1–2 ms pulse. 0805 is still an electrical requirement (64 mW on 0603 is 103 % of its 60 mW derated limit).

Cost: **+1 part number** (keeping 220R for the LEDs); or put 1 kΩ on all six for **zero new part numbers**, at the cost of 0.7–1.7 mA LED current (visible on the bench; the document itself says the LEDs are invisible in flight configuration and the buzzer is the status channel). Under the delete-parts principle, the latter is recommended.

### P1-6 IWDG rework + reset recovery (the "1600×" is a false margin)

* In the document's "timeout ≥ 2 s, 1600× margin", 1600× is the **ratio of blind-flight time to loop period** — making it bigger only makes things worse. The real timeout band is **1904.8–2150.5 ms** (LSI 29.76–33.60 kHz): 430 m of blind flight at 200 m/s, 738 m at Mach 1, and TIM5's CCRs are latched in hardware: **during a hang all four control surfaces hold their last command**. Full deflection at high dynamic pressure can produce roll accelerations on the order of 10³ deg/s² ⚠ (control effectiveness for this airframe **does not exist in the design document at all** and must be added); in 2.15 s that reaches ~4300 deg/s — past the gyro's full scale and possibly past structural and recovery limits. 30 ms only reaches 60 deg/s. **This is not "67× less blind flight"; it is the difference between a recoverable transient and loss of control.**
* **Change**: PR = /4, RL = 239 → 28.57–32.26 ms; move the feed point from the DRDY ISR to a **1 kHz TIM6 supervisor ISR (lowest preemption priority)** that feeds only if every subsystem heartbeat counter has advanced. False-trip margin 19.05× (still > 10×), detection latency 66.7× better.
* ⚠ **IWDG_WINR must be 235, never 200.** The original plan's WIN = 200 at PR = /4, RLR = 239 blocks every feed within 4.643–5.242 ms, while the feed cadence is 1.000 ms (the counter is still at 230.6–231.6) — **every feed triggers a window reset and the board reboots forever on the pad**. WIN = 235 blocks 0.476–0.538 ms, 1.86–2.10× margin against the 1 ms cadence, and still catches a runaway fast loop at > 2×. Also state honestly: at a 1 ms cadence the window only catches > 2× runaways — small value, but free.
* ⚠ The heartbeat table **must include a thread-level liveness counter**. If every counter in it is ISR-driven, TIM6 keeps feeding while the thread is stuck in while(1) — trading a "sensor drop-out blind spot" for a "thread hang blind spot".
* **Reset recovery**: DS12110 §3.29 verbatim: "the RTC and the 32 backup registers … are not reset by a system reset or power reset"; VBAT is copper-tied to VDD and the BOR0 falling edge (1.58–1.68 V) is above the 1.2 V backup-domain minimum, so the registers are intact at the moment of reset. Store 40 B (magic + phase + T0 + launch-site altitude + arm state + reset count + quaternion + CRC32). At boot read RCC_RSR, and **restore ARMED only if all four hold**: magic valid ∧ phase is BOOST/COAST ∧ reset source is BORRSTF/PINRSTF (not watchdog or software reset) ∧ baro altitude more than 100 m above the launch site. **The permanent-disarm rule for the pad phases (IDLE/ARMED_ON_PAD) stays untouched** — that is where the injury risk lives. ⚠ The hold-up window must be written down and measured: from 1.6 V to 1.2 V on 49 µF is only 27 µJ, a few milliseconds at a few mA of load, the same order as the 2.46 ms hold-up it is meant to back up; therefore the 40 B **must** also be mirrored into every 16th flash log record.
* Recovery budget (checker-corrected): 32.258 ms detection (slow LSI corner) + **6.020 ms for NRST's 100 nF to charge through the internal 30–50 kΩ** (which eats all of the original report's "~5 ms boot") + 0.5 POR + 0.1 clocks + 1.0 peripheral re-attach + 1.25 first IMU sample + 9.04 first baro sample = **50.2 ms ≈ 12.5 m @ 250 m/s**. ⚠ The recovery path must run on HSI 64 MHz and **must never wait for the HSE** (CubeMX's default HSE_STARTUP_TIMEOUT = 100 ms alone would triple the budget).
* The side effect must be written down in black and white: **this deliberately weakens the strongest safety rule on the board.** The baro altitude gate is the only thing stopping a "re-arm on the ground", so the MS5611 PROM CRC-4 check is upgraded from "anti-counterfeit good habit" to a **safety-critical function**. Two consecutive brown-outs re-arm twice.

### P1-7 Log rate table + page buffering

Three rates (pad 1 Hz heartbeat / 500 Hz after lift-off detection with a hard 25–40 s window / 50 Hz after apogee): 1.59 MB = **10.55×**, zero hardware. Forbid partial-page programming (one 64 B record per PP = 2.00 ms vs tPP 3 ms max = **0.67×, passes on the bench, fails hot**, and writing the same page four times is 4× P/E stress for zero benefit). Full 256 B pages + a **32 KB AXI-SRAM ring buffer** (**not in DTCM**: DS12110 §3.3 says TCM is accessible only by the CPU or MDMA; DMA1/DMA2 cannot reach it) → absorbs a 1.024 s stall = 341× tPP max, 2.56× a 400 ms sector erase. Add two rules nobody had written: **before arming, confirm SR1.BUSY = 0 and spot-read the log area as 0xFF** (tCE is 40 s typ / 200 s max; arming immediately after an erase means every later PP is silently rejected and the entire flight log vanishes without a trace); **every record carries a 2 B sync word + 2 B CRC16** (6.25 % overhead, buying resync after a brown-out tear and the ability to tell corrupt records from real ones — the board's entire accident-investigation capability rests on this log).

### P1-8 Quiet the servos before firing + LVC 6.6 V (free, and the biggest single improvement on the brown-out axis)

In the self-consistent solution for 6.0 V cut-off, four servos stalled and firing at the same time: V_RAW = (6.0 − 0.083 × 7.48) / (1 + 0.083 / 0.8995) = 4.93 V; after D-LOG, VLOGIC ≈ **4.58 V vs the AP63203's 3.8 V = 1.20×**. The 470 µF **does not help at all** — the droop lasts the whole 20 ms, the diode conducts throughout, and the bulk capacitor never gets to discharge. The same case drags fire margin from 5.8× to 5.0×.

**Change**: before firing, command all four servos to their **measured unloaded centre**, wait the measured settling time, then fire. With the servos quiet, VLOGIC ≈ 5.13 V at the 6.0 V LVC = **1.35×**; with the LVC raised to 6.6 V it is **1.50×**, fire margin returns to 6.1×, and worst-case bus current falls from 13.57 to 6.09 A (load-switch current margin 1.25× → 2.79×). Losing roll control for 20–30 ms at apogee costs nothing (ballistic phase, dynamic pressure near zero).

⚠ **Do not "write CCR = 0".** CCR = 0 means signal loss to a servo, and signal-loss behaviour is servo firmware, not a datasheet parameter; MKS publishes nothing for the HV6120: some digital servos go limp, some hold the last position and keep fighting the hinge moment, some slew to a stored default (**more transient current**). The whole 6.09 A figure rests on unverified behaviour. Measure it with a current probe under a representative hinge load — an hour's work.

### P1-9 Copper and vias

* **Keep but raise** §5.1's "RAW copper ≥ 6.1 mm equivalent": `RAW and servo GND: from J-BAT through Q1/Q2 to J-SERVO, L1 ≥ 10 mm equivalent, mirrored and stitched on L4; 6.1 mm is the absolute minimum (IPC-2221, 12 A, 20 K rise), allowed only locally at pad necks. At 10 mm the rise is 5.6 K at 12 A and 1.9 K at 7.48 A.` **Do not delete the 6.1 mm rule** (its derivation is correct; deleting it leaves only a temperature-rise statement a layout agent cannot execute).
* ⚠ **Delete the 3 mm track fallback in PCB_PIPELINE.md**: 3 mm on outer 1 oz at 20 K rise = 7.20 A, 0.96× of the 7.48 A written in the same cell.
* ⚠ **§1's "a 1 oz, 40 mm inner plane already carries 17.3 A per IPC-2221"**: the arithmetic is exact (2170 mil² → 17.34 A), but 2170 mil² is a **3.1× extrapolation** beyond the chart's 700 mil² valid range; the number cannot be falsified and should not appear in the design. The side effect that really matters: **JLCPCB's default inner copper is 0.5 oz; 40 mm of 0.5 oz = 10.49 A** — the 1 oz inner upgrade the document requires is a **paid option that must be selected explicitly at order time and verified on the fab drawing**; missing it is a silent 2× loss. This is the single most valuable action item of the whole review.
* Vias: **the best answer is 0 series vias** — J-BAT / Q1, Q2 / J-SERVO are already in the same 25 × 40 mm corner (as the document requires), so keep all 7.48 A on L1. Then ≥ 40 vias of 0.3 mm / 25 µm as **parallel stitching** (3.6×, 25.6 mm²; 112 vias = 10.08×, 71.7 mm²); in parallel, one cracked via is graceful degradation rather than an open circuit. ⚠ Each stitching via punches an antipad in L2, and the document forbids breaking L2 — **confine the via field strictly to the battery corner (y 0–20 mm)**, where L2 is a return path rather than the IMU/GPS reference plane.
* Pyro traces **must be treated adiabatically, not with IPC-2221**: 1 mm wide 1 oz rises ~6 K at 8.82 A / 20 ms and ~29 K at 84 A / 1 ms — huge margin. Write that sentence into the document so nobody later "fixes" the pyro traces to satisfy a rule that does not apply.

### P1-10 Move BUZZ_GATE off PE6

PE4/PE5/PE6 are LQFP100 **pins 3/4/5, physically adjacent**: PYRO1, PYRO2, buzzer. A PE5–PE6 solder bridge feeds the buzzer's ~2.7 kHz square wave (370 µs period) into the PYRO2 gate; with today's 10 nF (τ = 4.7 µs) the gate follows the drive completely and **the channel fires**. And **the 220 nF recommended in §4.1 makes this defect worse, not better** (τ = 103 µs against a 185 µs half-period; the gate settles at 1.0–2.6 V and the FET sits half-on). A PE4–PE5 bridge fires both channels. There are still ~41 free GPIOs; moving BUZZ_GATE to **another port on the other side of the package** is free and removes a single-solder-defect → inadvertent-fire path. Neither the document nor any original report discussed gate-pin adjacency.

### P1-11 Non-blocking I2C and GPS (both deadline violations live here)

One polled MS5611 read is 56 bit-times @ 400 kHz = **140 µs = 11.2 % of the loop**, dragging 82× down to 8.1× and making the USART6 overrun margin **0.56× (characters will be lost)**. Worse, the MS5611 datasheet warns verbatim that "SDA held by the module in the acknowledge state" is a real state whose only way out is "several SCLKs + a reset sequence" — **a blocking read never returns from that state**, and under the current watchdog architecture that is a lose-the-rocket chain. Change: I2C1 interrupt/DMA only, each transaction with a 3× nominal timeout on the TIM2 microsecond timebase; timeout → 9-clock bus recovery → MS5611 RESET (2.8 ms PROM reload) → still failing → BARO_FAILED, degrade to the timer backup. Conversions **must never be waited on** (OSR4096 = 9.04 ms max = 7.2 loop periods); it must be a "start conversion / return / collect later" state machine, which caps the effective baro rate at **110.6 Hz**. GPS moves to **circular DMA + IDLE line** (per-byte interrupts are 11,520 per second; ⚠ the checker pointed out that the real jitter source is not those — a low-priority interrupt cannot preempt a high-priority one — but **any global interrupt-disable / BASEPRI critical section**, so the specification must hard-code "longest interrupts-disabled region ≤ 10 µs", measurable with a GPIO toggle).

### P1-12 TIM5 CCR preload (a free control-surface glitch)

`OCxPE = 1` + `CR1.ARPE = 1`, all four CCRs written in one ISR and latched on the same update event. An 800 Hz loop writes asynchronously 2.4 times per 333 Hz frame; one write landing between old and new values misses the compare and the output stays high until the end of the frame — **a full-authority control-surface glitch of up to 3 ms**. Also record two facts missing from the document: **the servo command rate is 333 Hz, not 800 Hz** (1.4 of every 2.4 updates are discarded); **actuation-chain transport delay is 7–11.75 ms** (sensor filter group delay + sample/compute + PWM frame 0–3 ms + servo internal 3–5 ms), which caps the roll-loop crossover at ~10 Hz, i.e. the 800 Hz loop is **oversampled 80×** relative to achievable bandwidth — one more reason to reject 480 MHz, and it shows the only delay worth buying back is writing CCR just before the TIM5 update event (saves ~1.5 ms, 13–21 % of the budget, zero parts).

### P1-13 Three process items (zero BOM, covering the real world's top failures)

* **Conformal coating is mandatory**, focused on the pyro block and the SWD header, with connectors masked. The calculable reason: 8.4 V leaking across the surface to a pyro gate needs only **~12 kΩ** to reach the 0.65 V Vgs(th) min (12.9 kΩ after §4.1) — a few millimetres of damp FR-4 with black-powder residue can do that. The second exposure is **BOOT0**: a 10k pull-down, with the SWD header's 3V3 two positions away on the same connector; a residue bridge of the same order makes the next power-up silently enter the ROM bootloader — firmware never runs, the pyros are never armed, silent loss of the vehicle.
* **Staking of tall parts** (NASA-STD-8739.1 / IPC-A-610): two Ø8 × 10 mm, ~1.8–2 g 470 µF cans with their centre of mass 5–6 mm above the board stand on two SMD terminals. Static load is not the issue (0.31 MPa at 40 g vs 20 MPa is 65×); **resonant fatigue** is: a 1.8 g cantilever resonates at 1–3 kHz, squarely inside the 20–2000 Hz random-vibration band, and with Q = 10–20 a 14 Grms input becomes 140–280 g at the capacitor; the credible failure is pad lift / pad cratering. Two beads of RTV or epoxy fillet turn the cantilever into a fixed support and push the resonance up 3–5× out of band. The SAM-M10Q (~3 g, 6.3 mm tall, LGA with no lead compliance) gets **four corner dots**, not full underfill — it is the most expensive line on the BOM ($22.55, 51 pcs in stock), and corner staking remains reworkable.
* **Positive retention of the servo plugs**: 2.54 mm friction plugs have no latch; retention is 1.5–6 N per plug ⚠ (generic figures, not from this part's datasheet). A plug + 300 mm of free lead (~6 g) at 100 g is 5.89 N = **0.3×–1.0×, it will come off**. The design already has **one** 3 mm zip-tie hole; **drill a second on the other side of the header** so the tie passes **over** all four seated plugs and becomes the load path: a 3 mm nylon tie at 80 N vs 5.89 N is **13.6×** — the only place in this document where a 10× is both meaningful and nearly free on the mechanical side. Then write it into the assembly documentation as a **requirement**, not a suggestion (a hole nobody uses is no hole).

### P1-14 Board-level acceptance test (mandatory, before any real igniter)

Replace each igniter with a **10 Ω / 1 W resistor + ammeter**, arm, and confirm zero current in three states: ① MCU unpowered ② MCU held in reset ③ MCU running idle. **No board may touch a real igniter before passing this.** It covers in one go: the uninspectable DFN3×3 G–D solder bridge, Miller self-turn-on, gate leakage, residue leakage, and a FET already burnt into a short. Zero cost.

## 8. Net impact

### 8.1 ⚠ Outline conflict — must be resolved before any board is released

The brief says **40 × 115–120 mm, coupler ID 54.66 mm**; `ROCKET_FC_FROZEN_DESIGN.md` §1/§2.1 froze **50.0 × 110.0 mm, 75 mm minimum airframe, coupler ID 72.87 mm**, and ruling B5 declared a 54 mm airframe "unworkable". **Neither can be taken at face value**:

* B5's arithmetic assumes a **54 mm outer diameter** (ID 50.72 mm), where a 40 mm chord leaves 2 × √(25.36² − 20²) = **31.19 mm** of height, less than the 41.6 mm stack → fails.
* The brief gives **ID 54.66 mm**, where the same 40 mm chord leaves 2 × √(27.33² − 20²) = **37.25 mm**. The tallest fitted part is a 14 mm screw terminal. **B5 does not apply in height; the brief's airframe is geometrically feasible.**
* **But 40 mm of width raises a question B5 did not answer**: the end-edge budget is J-BAT 22.86 mm + 3×4 header 11.0 mm = **33.86 mm**, written as "of 50 mm" for a 50 mm board; on a 40 mm board, minus two Ø7.0 mm M3 washers, it has to be re-ruled.
* A length of 115–120 mm is looser than the frozen 110 mm; no problem.

**Every electrical conclusion in this prescription holds for both outlines** (DFN3×3 is no larger than SOT-23, the tallest part is still a terminal), but every layout/copper conclusion assumed 50 × 110 mm. **Answer this first.** *(Resolved in GEOMETRY_REV_G.md: 46 mm wide in a 54.66 mm ID airframe.)*

### 8.2 ⚠ Servo current baseline conflict — decides the fuse and the copper

The design document says 12 A main bus in **six places**; §2.3 makes "3 A per pin → each servo's stall ≤ 3 A" a hard servo-selection constraint, while §8 item 2 lists the servo model as **unconfirmed**. The brief's MKS HV6120 gives 7.48 A. **The two numbers cannot both be used**: either freeze the HV6120 into the document and rewrite the bus as 7.48 A (then §4.3 uses a 15 A fuse and 10 mm of copper still has 5.6 K of rise margin at 12 A), or keep the 12 A rule (then use a 20 A fuse, and 6.1 mm of copper at 0.74× must be raised to 10 mm). Auditing against a third number that exists in neither place is the only approach that cannot be right. *(GEOMETRY_REV_G.md later froze the HV6120 at 1.916 A per servo at 8.4 V, 7.66 A total.)*

### 8.4 Six measurements to make before touching anything (each one changes a ruling above)

1. **Battery pack DC internal resistance** (5 A pulse load; any balance charger reports it) — every brown-out number scales linearly with it; 1.00× (real loss of vehicle) appears at **64 mΩ/cell**, which needs **obvious cycle ageing and sub-zero temperature at the same time**, not something a healthy 25C pack reaches. Pre-flight go/no-go: **≤ 45 mΩ/cell**.
2. **Standby current and actual behaviour of the four servos with no pulse train** (30 seconds with a multimeter + one hour with a current probe) — decides whether battery margin is 18× or 4×, and whether §4.8's 6.09 A is real.
3. **Av-bay ambient temperature** (drop a logger in on a hot range day, ~$12) — the 70 °C assumption manufactured almost every "temperature failure" in this audit; measure before painting.
4. **Read or measure the AO3400A Fig 11 Zth (20 ms)** — the highest-leverage single number in the document; it decides whether the AON7524 is an "optional upgrade" or "required to comply".
5. **Measure the servo-bus ripple current** — the 470 µF's 2.19× is computed against a rating with no maker datasheet.
6. **Measure the DRDY ISR cycle count with DWT_CYCCNT** — the 82× is an estimate.
