# Rocket Flight Computer v1 — Schematic Electrical Defect Report

---

## 1. Overall verdict

# Must change

The drawing itself is of high quality: 78 nets and 370 connections checked endpoint by endpoint against frozen §5 with **zero miswires**; every chip pin mapping (ICM-45686, MS5611, W25Q128, SAM-M10Q, USBLC6, SRV05-4, AP63203, AON6403, AO3400A, USB-C, and all 55 used STM32H743 pins) verified **correct** against primary datasheets; four of the six "unreviewed decisions" judged correct. The verdict is driven by four things that must change before the copper is fixed: **(1) PE4/PE5 are TRACED1/TRACED2 — both pyro gates sit on the ETM synchronous trace port; tick Trace once in CubeMX and the CPU instruction stream drives the pyro gates, firing both channels on the bench with the arming plug in**; (2) the servo 220R was sized assuming the pin is an input / high-impedance, but in flight PA0–PA3 are **outputs driven low**, where the SRV05-4 does not conduct at all, so an 8.4 V chafe fault dumps 29.5–34.3 mA into a GPIO with a 20 mA absolute maximum while putting 191–259 mW into a 125 mW 0805; (3) VBUS has no pull-down at all; D3 (SS34) reverse leakage floats VBUS up to the USBLC6 clamp knee, so PD15 **always reads high** on battery power with no cable, and firmware constraint §7.11 never takes effect; (4) IMU_INT1 has no pull, while the ICM-45686's INT1 reset default is **open-drain active-low**, and this line is the IWDG's only feed point — when the IMU dies PE10 floats, and noise can feed the watchdog on the dead IMU's behalf.

One more safety fact must be written into the documentation: **the claim "a low-side FET shorting is a safe failure" is wrong**; it is a dangerous failure (see §2.2).

## 2. Safety defects

### 2.1 [Highest priority] PE4/PE5 are TRACED1/TRACED2 — enabling Trace drives both pyro gates with the CPU instruction stream

**Nets involved**: `PYRO1_GATE_N (R16.1, U1.3 = PE4)`, `PYRO2_GATE_N (R20.1, U1.4 = PE5)`; downstream `PYRO1_GATE (C35.1, Q3.1, R16.2, R17.1)`, `PYRO2_GATE (C36.1, Q4.1, R20.2, R21.1)`

**Mechanism**: the AF0 column of the DS12110 alternate-function table: PE2 = TRACECLK, PE3 = TRACED0, **PE4 = TRACED1, PE5 = TRACED2**, PE6 = TRACED3. This board fills the entire 5-pin synchronous trace port: U1.1 (PE2) = QSPI_IO2, U1.2 (PE3) = LED_RED, U1.3 (PE4) = PYRO1 gate, U1.4 (PE5) = PYRO2 gate, U1.5 (PE6) = BUZZ gate. Selecting `SYS → Debug → Trace Synchronous Sw 4 bits` in CubeMX (one drop-down) generates a `HAL_MspInit` that configures PE2–PE6 as AF0 push-pull at every power-up; J-Trace / ULINKpro / Ozone do the same from the debugger side.

The gate network is a divider: Vgs = D × VDD × 1000/1470 = 2.245·D, with time constant τ = (470 ‖ 1000) × (C35 10 nF + Ciss 630 pF) = 3.4 µs. At 50 % trace-stream duty the gate settles at **1.12 V**; the AO3400A's datasheet minimum VGS(th) is **0.65 V** (not the frozen document's 0.4 V), giving 0.47 V of overdrive; extrapolating from the datasheet's own VGS = 2 V curve (a typical part at Vov 0.95 V saturates at ~10 A) by Vov² gives **~2.4 A** → 5.8 W into a 1 Ω bridgewire, far beyond any e-match's all-fire. And TPIU sync and idle frames contain continuous runs of 1s; any high longer than ~10 µs (3τ) charges the gate to the full 2.24 V, **5.9 A**.

Two related problems: PE2 = QSPI_IO2 means enabling Trace also collides with the flash data bus; PB3 (U1.89 = SPI1_SCK, the IMU clock) is TRACESWO/JTDO, so choosing the more common `Trace Asynchronous Sw` kills the IMU SPI clock — and since the IWDG is fed only in the IMU DRDY ISR, that becomes a watchdog reset loop.

**Fix**: move both pyro gates to **PE0 (LQFP100 pin 97) / PE1 (pin 98)**. All four properties verified: both are `I/O FT_h` (7.3 V input limit, positive injection impossible — strictly better than PE4/PE5's current "unverified" entries); both have `—` in the AF0 column (no system/trace function); neither is in AN2606's H74x bootloader pin set; neither is used on this board (the netlist uses only 55 U1 pins, and 97/98 are not among them). Two net changes, zero BOM change.

If PE4/PE5 are kept, it must be written as a hard constraint: `SYS Debug` may only be Serial Wire, no build configuration may enable the TPIU, and startup code must explicitly re-drive PE4/PE5 push-pull low after `HAL_MspInit`.

*(FMEA_REDUNDANCY.md H-2 and DESIGN_ASSURANCE.md §4.6 reached different pin conclusions; reconcile them in the M3 pin table before merging the pyro circuit.)*

### 2.2 Q3/Q4 are the **only** series break between the pyro bus and the igniters — a drain-source short fires the channel the moment the arming plug is inserted

**Nets involved**: `PYRO_BUS (J4.2, J4.3, J4.5, R14.1)`, `PYRO1_OUT (J4.4, Q3.3, R18.1)`, `Q3.2 on GND`; channel 2 mirrored, `PYRO2_OUT (J4.6, Q4.3, R22.1)`

**Mechanism**: the arming loop is `RAW (J4.1) → external fused arming plug → J4.2/J4.3 → igniter → PYRO1_OUT → Q3.3 → Q3.2 → GND`. No high-side device, no second break. With Q3 shorted drain to source, the loop closes the instant the plug is inserted: 8.4 V across a 1–2 Ω bridgewire plus about 28 mΩ of FET and wiring = **4.2–8.4 A**, about four orders of magnitude above an MJG-type no-fire. A human hand is holding the plug.

**An incorrect claim written into the previous round's conclusions must be corrected**: "a FET short is a safe failure, because PYRO*_CONT reads 0 V and §7.4 will refuse to arm". That confuses **firmware arming** with **physical arming**. In this design arming is a person screwing a plug containing a 5 A fuse onto J4 pins 1/2; firmware has no vote in that. `PYRO*_CONT` reading 0 V is an **autopsy after the fact**, not an interlock in the loop. The gate pull-down, the 470R gate resistor and the 10 nF Miller capacitor only defend against "the FET being partially turned on", not against "the FET is already shorted". Also, even with the master switch open, RAW takes 53 seconds to fall from 8.4 V to 4 V (see §2.5).

**Fix**: no mandatory circuit change (a single low-side FET is the usual topology in amateur HPR), but it must be **explicitly recorded as an accepted risk**, with procedures and acceptance tests added:
1. Add a line to §8: "Q-PY1/Q-PY2 are the only series break between PYRO_BUS and each igniter; a drain-source short fires that channel when the ARM plug is inserted. Accepted risk."
2. Hard-code the pad procedure: the ARM plug goes in **last**, after personnel have cleared, and comes out **first** during recovery.
3. Add a bench acceptance test to §7: insert the ARM plug with a 2 Ω power resistor in place of each igniter, command both gates low, and verify `PYRO1_CONT` / `PYRO2_CONT` read **0.76 V, not 0 V** — the only moment a shorted FET can be caught non-destructively.
4. If adding parts is acceptable: add a second series element on PYRO_BUS (a high-side P-FET or a mechanical safe/arm). That is a real design change and should be proposed as one, not assumed.

### 2.3 The pull-pin interlock **fails toward armed**, and PD14 does not appear anywhere in the fourteen firmware constraints of §7

**Nets involved**: `PULLPIN (R36.1, R37.1, U1.61 = PD14)`, `PULLPIN_EXT (J6.1, R37.2)`, `J6.2 on GND`, `R36.2 on 3V3`

**Mechanism**: frozen §3.9 states "pin inserted reads 0.30 V, pulled reads 3.30 V", i.e. **LOW = safe, HIGH = arming permitted**. Staying safe depends on one continuous conductive path leaving the board, going to the pin hole in the airframe wall and coming back. **Every possible failure of that path is an open, and every open reads HIGH**: J6 is a through-hole part and JLCPCB standard SMT does not place through-hole (a hand-soldered board might not have it at all), the pull-pin wire not plugged in, loosened by boost vibration, a broken wire, a cold crimp, an oxidised or deformed shorting link. All silently report "pin pulled".

§7's fourteen constraints cover servos, pyros, IMU, barometer, GNSS, ADC, USB, clocks and even the buzzer, **but never once mention PD14 / PULLPIN / debouncing / what the pull-pin gates**. With this hardware failure direction, "not written" is not undefined, it is dangerous: the most natural firmware (HIGH means arming allowed) is exactly the one that turns a wiring fault into a lost interlock.

Note also the coupling with §2.4: the same wire chafing onto 8.4 V both exceeds the FT absolute maximum and locks the interlock in the permissive state — one chafe does both.

**Fix** (firmware, zero cost, must be written as §7.15):
- Define the polarity: `PULLPIN` LOW (0.30 V) = pin inserted = safe; HIGH (3.30 V) = pin pulled.
- **Latch on the transition, not the level**: after power-up LOW must be observed continuously for ≥ 1 s before a later LOW → HIGH edge is accepted as "pulled". HIGH at power-up → interlock **failed**, refuse to arm, sound a dedicated buzzer code + LED pattern (§7.14 already has a tone vocabulary).
- Software debounce ≥ 20 ms on top of the hardware RC; timestamp every edge into the log, so afterwards you can check whether the interlock was really in place.
- State explicitly what the pull-pin gates: pin inserted **forbids** arming; pin pulled is **only a permission, never sufficient on its own**.

Optional hardware (fixing the failure direction itself): invert the topology — `R36` becomes a 10k pull-down to GND, `J6.2` moves from GND to 3V3, `R37` becomes 2.7k. An open then reads LOW = safe, and an 8.4 V chafe is held to 6.61 V (inside 7.3 V). The cost is only 1.12× VIH margin (2.60 V vs 2.31 V). A real trade-off: **either choice is fine, but which one was chosen must be recorded explicitly**.

### 2.4 An 8.4 V chafe on the pull-pin lifts PD14 to 7.94 V (FT absolute maximum 7.3 V), and it is the only unclamped off-board signal on the board

**Nets involved**: same as above

**Mechanism**: PD14 is an input, so the node is the divider of 8.4 V through R37 (1k) and 3.3 V through R36 (10k):
V = (8.4 × 10k + 3.3 × 1k) / 11k = **7.936 V**, 8.7 % above DS12110 Table 21's FT limit Min(VDD, VDDA, VDD33USB, VBAT) + 4.0 = **7.3 V**, and sustained indefinitely. Above 7.3 V the "positive injection impossible" premise of Table 58 no longer holds and injection behaviour is completely undefined.

ESD is worse: DS12110 Table 56 rates this silicon only to **VESD (HBM) = 1000 V (Class 1C)**, CDM 250 V. The pull-pin is the **only conductor on the whole rocket that a person deliberately grabs and yanks** seconds before launch, standing next to the rail, often on a dry day. An everyday 5–10 kV human discharge is 5–10× the pin's rating. R37 = 1k in series with the 1.5k HBM source resistance only cuts peak current to 60 %, meaningless against an 8 kV event. Every other off-board signal on the board has real protection (servos: 220R + an SRV05-4 rated ±8 kV IEC contact; VBUS: 100k); the pull-pin has one 1k.

Changing the resistor ratio cannot solve it (**while keeping the current polarity**): with k = R36/R37, the chafe requires (8.4k + 3.3)/(k + 1) ≤ 7.3 → k ≤ 3.64; pin inserted requires 3.3/(k + 1) ≤ VIL = 0.3·VDD = 0.94 V @ VDD 3.135 V → k ≥ 2.51. A window exists, but its best point k ≈ 3.1 gives only 1.17× on VIL and 2 % on the limit — neither usable.

**Fix** (one of two, considered together with §2.3's hardware option):
- **Add a second SRV05-4 (C13612, already a BOM part number: 0 new part numbers, +1 placement)**: pin 5 → 3V3, pin 2 → GND, one I/O → PULLPIN. A chafe clamps the node to 3V3 + VF = 4.1–4.5 V, current (8.4 − 4.3)/1k = 4.1 mA, absorbed by the 3V3 rail load; ESD is taken by a part rated ±8 kV IEC contact. **The remaining three channels fit exactly the three unprotected off-board signals on J8** (`SPARE_ADC (J8.5, U1.34)`, `I2C1_SCL (J8.3)`, `I2C1_SDA (J8.4)`) — one part closes two problems.
- Or adopt §2.3's inverted topology, fixing both the chafe and the failure direction without adding parts.
- If neither is done: the pull-pin harness must be < 150 mm of twisted pair, physically separated from the servo/pyro harness (other side, different ties), and this must be written as an **assembly instruction**, not a preference.

### 2.5 ARM_SENSE and both PYRO*_CONT have no filter capacitor — three ADC nodes hanging directly on an unshielded pyro harness

**Nets involved**: `ARM_SENSE (R14.2, R15.1, U1.33 = PC5)`, `PYRO1_CONT (R18.2, R19.1, U1.15 = PC0)`, `PYRO2_CONT (R22.2, R23.1, U1.16 = PC1)`

**Mechanism**: all three are 10k/1k dividers with a 909 Ω Thevenin source and **no capacitance to ground at all**. By contrast, the purely on-board node `VBAT_SENSE (C12.1, R3.2, R4.1, U1.32)` does have C12 (10 nF). The filtering went on exactly the wrong node.

`PC5` is confirmed **TT_a**: DS12110 Table 21 absolute maximum `Input voltage on TT_xx pins = VSS − 0.3 … 4.0 V`; the **first row** of the Table 59 injection-susceptibility table is `PA7, PC5, PG1, PB14, PJ7, PA11–PA15, PJ12, PB4`, 5 mA negative injection, **0 mA positive** — the class of pin on the whole device least tolerant of positive injection. Stricter still, the General operating conditions table caps TT_xx operation at VDD + 0.3 ≈ **3.6 V**. With an 11:1 divider, only **40 V** on PYRO_BUS lifts PC5 to 3.6 V.

The frozen document's only transient analysis of PC5 was "even with the SMBJ clamping at 19.9 V this node is only 1.81 V" — but the SMBJ12CA sits across `BAT+ (D1.1)` and `GND (D1.2)` and handles surges from the battery leads; it does nothing for transients induced on or discharged into **the pyro harness itself**. And PYRO_BUS and both igniter returns are exactly the unshielded wires that leave J4 and run the length of the airframe. When a bridgewire burns open, the arming-plug lead inductance kicks the bus up, or a person discharges static into an igniter lead — 40 V is not an exaggeration. All four SRV05-4 channels are taken by the servos (`D5.1/D5.3/D5.4/D5.6`), so there is no clamp channel left (frozen §8 item 6 admits as much).

(Note: the previous round proposed "during turn-off avalanche PYRO_BUS sits one igniter drop above the drain = 43.5 V → PC5 3.96 V". That argument has the inductance term's sign backwards — lead inductance is what kicks the **drain** up, and its bus side is at the lower potential; PYRO_BUS is the inductive divider tap between RAW and the clamped drain, bounded by the clamp level itself. Also, the 470R + 10 nF deliberately slow turn-off (τ = 3.4 µs), so a commanded turn-off only takes the drain to ~11.5 V and never avalanches. The real exposure is **the arc when a bridgewire burns open** driving the bus, which agrees with the conclusion above.)

**Fix**: add one **10 nF to GND** each on `ARM_SENSE`, `PYRO1_CONT` and `PYRO2_CONT` (reusing the BOM's existing C57112 0603 10 nF line, 3 → 6). Each τ = 909 Ω × 10 nF = 9.1 µs, over 2000× faster than a 20 ms fire pulse, with no effect on the present/absent decision, while dividing a ~1 MHz bus spike 10k : 16 Ω — a 40 V bus transient leaves 64 mV at the pin, 625× attenuation. It also improves ADC settling from the 909 Ω source. Then add a "harness transient" column to the PC0/PC1/PC5 rows in §3.3 (today there is only the battery-side TVS column), and mark these four divider capacitors (C12 + the three new ones) "safety-related, do not delete" in §4.2 with their τ values.

### 2.6 PYRO_BUS / PYRO1_OUT / PYRO2_OUT have no bypass, TVS or clamp at all

**Nets involved**: `PYRO_BUS (J4.2, J4.3, J4.5, R14.1)`, `PYRO1_OUT (J4.4, Q3.3, R18.1)`, `PYRO2_OUT (J4.6, Q4.3, R22.1)`

**Mechanism**: the only components on these three nets are one MOSFET drain and one 10k resistor. All leave through 5.08 mm screw terminals and run 0.5–1 m to the igniters. Two exposures:
- **ESD**: an operator on a dry pad holds an igniter lead and discharges into PYRO1_OUT. The only clamp is Q3's own avalanche at 30 V minimum, and **AOS Rev 3.1's AO3400A electrical characteristics table has no EAS/IAS row at all** (frozen §4.5 used exactly that to argue the buzzer needs a freewheel diode). An 8 kV HBM event is about 5 mJ, two orders of magnitude above the 23–82 µJ the part sees in a normal turn-off avalanche. The result is a degraded or shorted pyro FET — and per §2.7 a shorted pyro FET is **undetectable in any state** in this circuit, and then it fires when the arming plug is inserted.
- **RF**: HPR sites have handheld radios and launch-control telemetry, and a 1 m igniter lead is a good VHF/UHF antenna. Bypassing the igniter with a capacitor on the firing unit is common practice in ordnance handling.

For scale: this design spends a whole part (SRV05-4) protecting four servo signals carrying only 3.3 V logic, and another (1N5819WS) on the buzzer coil, while **the two nets actually connected to explosives have nothing**.

**Fix**: add **10–100 nF / ≥ 50 V / X7R to GND** on each of `PYRO1_OUT` and `PYRO2_OUT`, placed at J4. Two 0603s. Doing the arithmetic for the likely objection: inserting the arming plug charges it through the bridgewire at 8.4 V / 1 Ω with RC ≈ 100 ns, a total of 0.84 µC and 3.5 µJ, thousands of times below any e-match's no-fire energy; turning on dumps the same 3.5 µJ into the FET, against the ~26 µJ the FET already absorbs crossing its linear region; and it **reduces** the Miller kick at plug insertion (by slowing the drain edge), turning the single point in §2.7 into a non-single point. For a proper ESD path, add one SMAJ15CA-class bidirectional TVS across PYRO_BUS–GND — one more part — giving the AO3400A a clamp it does not have today.

### 2.7 C35/C36 are the only barrier against "firing when the arming plug is inserted", and they fail open silently and untestably

**Nets involved**: `C35.1 on PYRO1_GATE (with Q3.1, R16.2, R17.1)`, `C35.2 on GND`; `C36.1 on PYRO2_GATE`, `C36.2 on GND`

**Mechanism**: the circuit itself is right; this is a single-point-of-failure observation. With the plug bridging J4.1 ↔ J4.2 and an igniter present, `PYRO1_OUT` (the drain) steps from 0 to 8.4 V through a ~1 Ω bridgewire. The source is the 470 µF behind RAW, so the edge is limited only by plug and lead inductance (0.5 µH / 1 Ω → L/R 0.5 µs ≈ 17 V/µs), far faster than R17·C35's 10 µs, so charge conservation gives the bound. With the **measured datasheet** Qgd = 1.8 nC:
- **With C35**: ΔVgs = 1.8 nC / (10 nF + 0.63 nF Ciss) = **169 mV**, 3.8× margin against the 0.65 V VGS(th) minimum. Safe.
- **Without C35** (one cracked or open 0603 MLCC): ΔVgs = 1.8 nC / 630 pF = **2.9 V**, above the 1.45 V VGS(th) **maximum**. The FET turns fully on and the igniter goes off the instant the plug goes in, with a hand on the plug.

(Frozen §1 computed this margin as 6.8× / 95 mV using Crss 75 pF / Ciss 750 pF — the 75 pF is actually **Coss**, copied into the Crss cell. True values: Crss 50 pF, Ciss 630 pF, Qgd 1.8 nC. The protection is real and necessary, just not as thick as believed.)

**Fix**: the capacitors cannot be touched; they do exactly the job they were added for. Three hardening measures:
1. Specify C35/C36 as **soft-termination 0603 X7R, or 0805** — the dominant open-failure mode of MLCCs at 30 g is flex cracking.
2. A layout constraint, written as an **electrical requirement** so layout review picks it up: away from board edges, mounting holes and depaneling routes.
3. Adopt §2.6's output capacitors — they slow the drain edge at insertion to the millisecond range, where R17 alone divides the Miller current away (Crss × dV/dt × 1 kΩ is far below 1 mV), so the gate capacitor stops being a single point of failure for this hazard.

### 2.8 RAW does not discharge after the master switch is turned off — PYRO_BUS stays above 4 V for about a minute after power-off

**Nets involved**: `RAW (C1.1, C2.1, C3.1, D2.2, J4.1, J5.2/5/8/11, Q2.5–Q2.9, R3.1)`, `VBAT_SENSE (C12.1, R3.2, R4.1, U1.32)`, `R4.2 on GND`

**Mechanism**: with the servos unplugged, RAW's entire DC load is R3 (100k) + R4 (47k) = 147 kΩ. RAW capacitance is the 470 µF polymer plus two 22 µF 0805s (about 10 µF each left at 8.4 V bias), about 490 µF in total. τ = 147k × 490 µF = **72 s**; 8.4 V → 4 V takes 72 × ln(2.1) = **53 s**, → 1 V takes 153 s. Q2's body diode points RAW → FET_S, so an open load switch does not discharge it either.

This directly weakens frozen §1's safety argument for "the pyro bus must be taken after SW": "otherwise plugging the arming plug with the board off energises the pyro bus, and 8.4 V hits an ADC pin with VDD = 0 V through 10k". In the circuit as drawn that protection is not absolute; it is **only delayed by a minute or two**, while the operator's mental model after flipping the switch is "it's dead". During that window PYRO_BUS carries 8 V and two ADC pins are back-fed while the MCU is long dead (VLOGIC's 470 µF collapses in ~18 ms at a 200 mA MCU load; 3V3 disappears much sooner than RAW).

**Fix** (one of two):
- **Documentation + procedure**: add to §8 and the pad checklist: "after power-off wait 60 s before inserting or removing the ARM plug; RAW decay time constant 72 s".
- **Circuit change**: change the battery divider `R3/R4` from 100k/47k to **10k/4.7k**. τ drops to 7.2 s, 5.3 s to reach 4 V; **the divider ratio is unchanged, so every voltage in §3.3 stays as written**; the cost is 8.4 / 14.7k = 571 µA of standing drain (about 4 mAh over a 7-hour launch day, negligible for a 2S pack). Side benefit: PC4's Thevenin impedance drops from 32 kΩ to 3.2 kΩ, improving ADC settling. Both values are already Basic parts.

### 2.9 Continuity sensing cannot tell "good bridgewire" from "igniter terminals shorted" — silent no-deploy

**Nets involved**: `PYRO1_CONT (R18.2, R19.1, U1.15)`, `PYRO2_CONT (R22.2, R23.1, U1.16)`

**Mechanism**: `PYRO1_CONT = PYRO_BUS × 1k / (10k + 1k + Rbridge)`. A 2 Ω MJG bridgewire gives 8.4 × 1000 / 11002 = **763.57 mV**; shorted terminals (Rbridge = 0) give 8.4 × 1000 / 11000 = **763.64 mV**. A difference of **0.07 mV**. At 16 bits with a 3.3 V reference 1 LSB = 50 µV, so the two states differ by about 1.4 LSB, while the 1–2 Ω part-to-part and temperature spread of real bridgewires is already 76 µV. Indistinguishable in practice.

So the divider is a reliable **open** detector, not a bridgewire detector; yet §7.4 says "read PYRO*_CONT to confirm the bridgewire is present". Three of the five adjacent terminal pairs on J4 produce this undetectable state: positions 3-4 and 5-6 short one igniter each (`J4.3/J4.4 = PYRO_BUS/PYRO1_OUT`, `J4.5/J4.6 = PYRO_BUS/PYRO2_OUT`), and so does 4-5. A stray strand, a solder splash, a pinched chafed harness all read "channel healthy", and then deployment simply doesn't happen — the FET turns on, current bypasses the bridgewire, and the rocket comes in ballistic.

A related but **detectable** case: a bridge across J4 positions 1-2 bypasses the removable fused arming plug, leaving PYRO_BUS permanently live and unfused. ARM_SENSE sees this (it still reads 0.764 V with the plug out), and firmware must assert on it.

**Fix** (two documentation items + one firmware item, no circuit change):
1. Correct §7.4: this test **only detects opens, not shorts**; the pad checklist must include testing each igniter with a compliant low-current igniter tester **before** it is connected to the terminals.
2. Firmware: if the operator declares disarmed while `ARM_SENSE` reads above about 0.4 V, latch a fault and announce it on the buzzer — the only member of this failure family the hardware can see.
3. Record in §8: no cheap divider change can close the short-detection gap, because telling 2 Ω from 0 Ω needs a constant-current source or a divider so low that the bridgewire current approaches the no-fire limit.

## 3. Miswired / non-functional

### 3.1 The servo 220R was chosen for the wrong fault state: with the pin driven low the SRV05-4 doesn't conduct at all, and ~30 mA flows into a 20 mA GPIO

**Nets involved**: `SERVO1_MCU (D5.1, R24.1, R28.1, U1.22)` / `SERVO1_OUT (J5.1, R24.2)`; the other three identical: `SERVO2 (D5.3, R25.1, R29.1, U1.23 / J5.4, R25.2)`, `SERVO3 (D5.4, R26.1, R30.1, U1.24 / J5.7, R26.2)`, `SERVO4 (D5.6, R27.1, R31.1, U1.25 / J5.10, R27.2)`. Fault source = `J5.2/J5.5/J5.8/J5.11` on `RAW`.

**Mechanism**: frozen §1 and §4.5 set the 220R and the 0805 package from one number, and added the SRV05-4 because of it: a chafe short to 8.4 V gives 17.7–19.5 mA and 69–84 mW, because the SRV05-4 clamps the node at 3V3 + VF = 4.1–4.5 V. Recomputed, that arithmetic is exactly right — **but only for a pin that is high-impedance / an input**.

In flight PA0–PA3 are TIM5 PWM outputs. Worse, §7.2 requires the very first boot instructions to drive PA0–PA3 push-pull low and treats driven-low as the safe state, so the entire pad wait is **100 % driven low**, not 33–67 %. With the pin driven low the node is at V = 8.4 × Rds / (220 + Rds); DS12110 Table 60 gives VOL ≤ 0.4 V @ 8 mA and ≤ 1.3 V @ 20 mA, i.e. a worst-case low-side driver of **50–65 Ω**, typically ~25 Ω. The node sits at **0.9–1.9 V**, nowhere near 3.3 V + VF — **the SRV05-4's upper clamp diode is reverse-biased and carries no current at all**. All fault current returns into the MCU:

> I = 8.4 / (220 + 25…65) = **29.5 – 34.3 mA**, **148–172 %** of DS12110 Table 21's `IIO = 20 mA` absolute maximum, sustained indefinitely.
> P in R24 = I²R = **191–259 mW**, **153–207 %** of an 0805's 125 mW (0805 was chosen to hold it at 55–67 %).
> All four faulted at once: 118–137 mA out of VSS (pin 26) next to PA0–PA3, exceeding Table 21's `IVSS = 100 mA per pin`.

**Driving high** is no better: with the output PMOS on the pin is about 50 Ω to VDD, while the clamp diode needs 0.6–1.2 V to conduct; solving gives the node at about VDD + 0.9 V, with about 18 mA of the ~19 mA flowing **backwards through the MCU's own P-channel output transistor into VDD**, and only 1–2 mA through the clamp. **Conclusion: the SRV05-4 only protects the pin in the high-impedance state, when the MCU is dead or in reset.** (Good news: DS12110 Table 57 rates this silicon's latch-up at JESD78 II level A = ±100 mA, so 30 mA will not latch it up. But it is an unrated stress, not protection.)

The trigger needs nothing exotic: a chafe or crush between the signal and +RAW wires in the same three-wire servo lead (exactly the fault §1 / §4.5 imagined when sizing this resistor); a solder bridge or bent pin between adjacent J5 rows at 2.54 mm; a signal-to-supply short inside the servo.
(The previous round's "JR plug inserted rotated 180°" trigger does not hold: reversed, the servo's own signal-pin ESD diode clamps its floating ground rail about 0.7–4.9 V above board ground, and the MCU sinks only 3–19 mA, inside 20 mA; column-major numbering also makes cross-column insertion impossible. **The conclusion stands, but the reason must be chafes / solder bridges.**)

**Fix**: change `R24/R25/R26/R27` from 220R to **680R**. The sizing rule must be written down with its reason: **size R assuming the SRV05-4 does not exist, because the MCU's own output transistors (50–65 Ω) bypass the clamp in both drive states.**
- Driven-low fault: I = 8.4 / (680 + 25…65) = **11.3–11.9 mA** (57–60 % of 20 mA); P(R) = 87–96 mW.
- Driven-high fault: about 6.9 mA in reverse. High-impedance: the clamp works normally, about 6 mA.
- No loss of drive margin: Vservo = 3.29 × Rin / (Rin + 680) = 3.24 V @ 47k, 3.08 V @ 10k, **2.87 V @ 4.7k**, all above the VIH upper bound of 2.31 V inferred from the HV6120's 3.5 V minimum operating voltage (1.24×).
- Time constant: 680 Ω × ~50 pF of servo lead = 34 ns, irrelevant against a 1–2 ms pulse.

**An honest statement of cost**: this is **not** zero new part numbers. The BOM §4.2 220R line (C17557, 0805, qty 6) is shared with the two LED current limiters; changing R24–R27 **splits** that line and adds a new 680R 0805 LCSC line. The zero-new-part-number alternative is **two 220R in series per channel = 440R**: I = 16.6–18.1 mA (83–90 % of 20 mA), 61–72 mW each. It passes, but with only 10–17 % current margin and 4 more placements. **680R is recommended**: one more BOM line buys 40 % margin. *(MARGIN_PRESCRIPTION.md P0-5 recommends 1.0 kΩ for the same reason, landing exactly on the guaranteed 8 mA VOL point.)*

Temperature note: a 0805 thick-film 125 mW rating applies at 70 °C, derating linearly to zero at 155 °C. At an 85 °C summer pad inside a closed airframe about 103 mW is allowed, so 680R's 96 mW worst case is ~93 % of the derated value (not 77 % at 25 °C). Still passes, but the document must carry this assumption — the existing "55–67 %" also omitted temperature.

### 3.2 VBUS has no pull-down at all: D3 reverse leakage lifts the node, PD15 always reads high on battery power, and §7.11 is inverted

**Nets involved**: `VBUS (C28.1, D3.2, D4.5, J3.A4B9, J3.B4A9, R9.1)` → R9 (100k) → `VBUS_SENSE (R9.2, U1.62 = PD15)`

**Mechanism**: count every DC path on the VBUS net: C28 (4.7 µF ceramic, nA-level leakage), `D4.5` = the USBLC6-2SC6 Vbus pin (IRM 10 nA typ / 150 nA max @ 5.25 V), R9 (100k) to PD15 (an FT pin, Ileak ±250 nA with **no guaranteed sign**), and `D3.2` = the **anode** of an SS34. **Not a single resistor to ground.**

With the cable unplugged and battery power, `VLOGIC` ≈ 8.05 V and D3 is reverse-biased; its reverse leakage flows from cathode (VLOGIC) to anode (VBUS), **charging the floating VBUS**. Vishay's SS3x specifies IR max 0.5 mA @ 25 °C at the rated 40 V (20 mA at 100 °C), and its Fig. 4 falls only 1.5–2 decades from 100 % to 20 % of rated VR, so the credible worst case here at ~8 V reverse and 25 °C is **5–20 µA**, tens of µA at 50–60 °C in a closed airframe. That is 10–100× larger than every competing sink (the USBLC6's 150 nA, PD15's 250 nA). VBUS charges all the way up to the USBLC6 VBUS clamp knee (VBR 6 V min) and stops there, and R9 delivers that level to PD15 — **as long as the battery is connected and the board is at all warm, PD15 is a solid logic 1**.

Software cannot rescue it; both paths are blocked: DS12110 Table 60 note 7 says explicitly "to sustain a voltage above MIN(VDD, VDDA, VDD33USB) + 0.3 V the internal pull-up/pull-down must be disabled"; and even if allowed, 100k in series with RPD (30–50k) divides a real 5 V VBUS down to 1.15–1.67 V, below VIH = 0.47·VDD + 0.25 = 1.80 V — failing in the opposite direction. ES0392 Rev 5 §2.2.13 independently requires "the application must keep VIN below VDD to avoid I/O leakage", which the single-resistor scheme violates by design.

**Consequence**: `§7.11` "with no VBUS (PD15 low), do not enable the OTG stack at all, so its interrupts never disturb the 800 Hz control loop" is **never true** in flight. This is the one "net addition, removable" part in §1, and it buys none of what it was bought for. It also self-latches: once the OTG stack is enabled, the H7's internal D+ pull-up (RPUI 900/1250/1600 Ω) pulls D+ to 3.3 V, forward-biasing the USBLC6's I/O2 → Vbus steering diode and holding the VBUS node up, so the condition never clears.

**Fix**: turn R9 into a divider, **sized by the leakage budget, not by the USBLC6's 150 nA**. The constraints are 5 V × k > VIH 1.80 V (k ≥ 0.36) and Ileak × Rtot × k < VIL 0.99 V (Rtot < 2.75 V / Ileak, < 55 kΩ at 50 µA).

> **Change `R9` from 100k to 10k, and add a 10k from `VBUS_SENSE` to `GND`** (both from the existing C25744 10k 0402; no new values).
> Plugged: PD15 = 2.50 V, 1.39× over VIH 1.80 V and below VDD, satisfying ES0392 §2.2.13.
> Unplugged: ≤ 0.20 V at 20 µA of leakage, only 0.50 V even at a pessimistic 50 µA, both far below VIL 0.99 V.
> Plugged, it draws 250 µA from the **host** (not the battery), well inside the 2.5 mA suspend budget; with a 12 V fault PD15 sees only 6 V, still within FT's 7.3 V.

**Do not** use 100k/100k: 20 µA of leakage lifts PD15 to 2.0 V — exactly the false high it was meant to remove. BOM: 100k (C25741) 3 → 2, 10k (C25744) +2.

### 3.3 IMU_INT1 has no pull, while the ICM-45686's INT1 resets to open-drain active-low — and it is the IWDG's only feed point

**Nets involved**: `IMU_INT1 = U4.4 (ICM-45686 pin 4, INT1) ↔ U1.40 (PE10)`, only 2 connections on the net, no pull of any kind

**Mechanism**: DS-000489 Rev 1.1 §16.23 `INT1_CONFIG2` (address 0x18) **resets to 0x04**: bit 2 `INT1_DRIVE = 1` = **open drain**, bit 1 `INT1_MODE = 0` = pulsed, bit 0 `INT1_POLARITY = 0` = **active low**. The §4.1 note for pin 4 also states "the internal pull-up is **off** by default" (pins 1/2/3/7/10/11/12/13/14 are all on by default; pin 4 alone is off). So **in the device's reset configuration nothing on either end of this line defines a high level**: open drain can only pull low, the board has no pull-up, and PE10 has no reset pull. At most one EXTI edge is produced and the line never returns high. The hardware only works after firmware first changes INT1_CONFIG2 to push-pull (and flips the polarity to active high) — and §7's firmware constraints never mention INT1_CONFIG2.

**More serious, it fails open**: §7.1 feeds the IWDG **only** in the DRDY ISR, 800 Hz against a ≥ 2 s timeout. With the IMU missing, unpowered, a cracked pin-4 joint or an asynchronous reset, PE10 becomes a truly floating high-impedance EXTI input (FT_xx Ileak only ±250 nA), sitting millimetres from a 1.1 MHz switch node and a 12 A servo bus. Noise-triggered edges **keep feeding the watchdog on behalf of an IMU that is already dead**, dissolving the only liveness check in flight while the control loop runs on stale data. Giving the pin a defined level turns every one of those failures into "no edges → IWDG timeout → reset → permanent disarm per §7.4", which is the behaviour the whole architecture assumed.

**Fix**: **add a 10k from `IMU_INT1` to `GND` (a pull-down, not a pull-up)**, and have firmware configure INT1 as **push-pull + active high + pulsed** (`INT1_DRIVE = 0, INT1_POLARITY = 1, INT1_MODE = 0`).
- The resistor must define the **idle** state: active high takes a pull-down, active low a pull-up.
- Static cost: DS-000489 Table 5 gives a tINT pulse width of 100 µs typ, so at 800 Hz (1.25 ms frame) the line is high 8 % of the time: an average of **26 µA** through 10k (2.6 µA with 100k).
- **Do not use a 10k pull-up as the previous round suggested**: a pull-up with active high means the IMU pulls the line low for 92 % of every frame, 330 µA × 92 % = **304 µA continuous**, 72 % of the ICM-45686's own 420 µA 6-axis LN operating current, and on the LC-filtered 3V3A sensor rail at that.
- Firmware order: first `WHO_AM_I` (register 0x72, expect 0xE9) to confirm the part, **then** write INT1_CONFIG2, **only then** configure EXTI10.

Strictly, this item's severity is DEGRADED rather than WILL_NOT_WORK — the part has programmable internal INT1 pulls (`pads_int1_tp0_tp_pe_trim_d2a[0]` / `pads_int1_tp0_tp_pud_trim_d2a[0]`), and TDK's own reference circuits (Figs 5–10) show no external resistor, so the board works when the firmware is right. **But what is missing is the fail-safe property "stop feeding the watchdog when the IMU dies", and this line is the board's only liveness check. One 0402 for that is worth it.** *(FMEA_REDUNDANCY.md later removed the IWDG feed from this ISR altogether; the defined idle level is still wanted.)*

### 3.4 The electrical pairing of LS1 (MLT-8530)'s four pads could not be settled from library data — the worst branch is 3V3 shorted straight to ground

**Nets involved**: `3V3` includes `LS1.1`; `BUZZ_OUT (D6.2, LS1.2, Q5.3)`; `LS1.3` / `LS1.4` on no net

**Mechanism and conflicting evidence**: the EasyEDA footprint `BUZ-SMD_4P-L8.5-W8.5-P8.50-BR` has four 2.4 mm square pads at the corners of a 7.2 mm square: pad 1 (385.83, 314.17), pad 2 (414.17, 314.17), pad 3 (414.17, 285.83), pad 4 (385.83, 285.83). **Pads 1 and 2 share one edge, pads 3 and 4 the opposite edge.** Every TRACK in the footprint is on the top silkscreen (layer 3) and every SOLIDREGION on layers 12/99/100; **no copper connects any of the four pads** — they are electrically independent in the footprint. The symbol names only pin 1 as `+`; pins 2/3/4 are unnamed.

- **Supporting "drawn correctly"**: the dimension drawing in the Huaneng MLT-8530 datasheet labels two pads `+ Lead` / `- Lead` (on the same edge) and the other two `Dummy Pad` (on the sound-port side). That matches the geometry above (1 & 2 on one edge, 3 & 4 on the other), so `LS1.1/LS1.2` are the electrical pair and leaving `LS1.3/LS1.4` floating is correct. And even with + and − swapped between 1 ↔ 2, the MLT-8530 is electromagnetic (a coil, not piezo), driven with a unipolar square wave by a low-side FET, so the topology is fully equivalent; the only consequence is the coil field opposing the permanent-magnet bias → **lower sound pressure**, shorting nothing and damaging neither Q5 nor D6.
- **The branch that could not be excluded**: the `+ Lead / - Lead / Dummy Pad` labels on that drawing are vector-graphic text; one fetch could read them, another got only the title page. If that reading is wrong and the corners are really "top two pads = one terminal, bottom two pads = the other", then `LS1.1 = 3V3` and `LS1.2 = BUZZ_OUT` connect **3V3 straight to Q5's drain**: at about 50 % duty, 2700 times a second, driving the AP63203 into current limit through the AO3400A's 28 mΩ. 3V3 collapses, the MCU browns out, and §7.4 permanently disarms on reset — exactly the lose-the-rocket chain.

**Fix**: **do not try to settle it by opening the footprint again (that was already done and did not converge).** Two ways that do settle it, each minutes of work:
1. Measure continuity between the four pads of a real MLT-8530 with a multimeter (the coil is about 16 ± 3 Ω; dummy pads are open).
2. Ask the supplier for Huaneng's terminal drawing (`www.lcsc.com/datasheet/C94599.pdf` returns only the title page to a fetcher, and `datasheet.lcsc.com` 301-redirects back to it).

If 1 & 2 turn out to be the same terminal: change to `LS1.1 = 3V3`, `LS1.4 = BUZZ_OUT` (or 1 + 2 = 3V3, 3 + 4 = BUZZ_OUT). If it is left/right terminals: the drawing is right, and 3/4 may optionally be joined to 3V3 / BUZZ_OUT for extra solder strength.

**Cheap insurance while undecided**: leave a **DNP 0R jumper position** between `BUZZ_OUT` and `LS1.4` so the connection can be changed at assembly. Whatever the result, write the pin numbers into §5.5 — today that row only describes function ("one end of LS1", "other end of LS1 = 3V3"), which is exactly why the drawing cannot be checked against it.

## 5. Rulings on six decisions made during drawing without review

### (a) MS5611 SDO (pin 6) tied to GND — **usable but not recommended (rated MINOR, change suggested)**

The pin mapping itself is entirely right and checked (TE 06/2017 datasheet pin table: 1 VDD / 2 PS / 3 GND / **4, 5 CSB (a merged cell, "internal connection"; both pins must be at the same potential)** / 6 SDO (O) / 7 SDI-SDA / 8 SCLK; the EasyEDA C15639 symbol's pin names match the datasheet one to one, so the pin numbers are not a symbol artefact). PS to 3V3A selecting I2C is right, and both CSB pins to GND is right (the datasheet requires "must not float").

**The SDO pin is rated not recommended**: the datasheet lists its type as **O (output)**, specified push-pull in the DC table; the mode table says `PS High → I2C → Pins used: SDA`, with SDO only in the SPI column; the datasheet never endorses grounding it. The drawing's recorded reason, "tie to GND by convention (avoid floating inputs)", **is factually wrong — SDO is not an input**, and the no-floating-CMOS-inputs rule does not apply to it.

The hazard probability is low rather than unknown: the SPI section says "CSB is used to enable/disable the interface so other devices can share the same SPI bus", which presupposes SDO is high-impedance when disabled; and with PS tied high, SPI is never selected. But this is a $5.02 part with no substitute, frequently counterfeited, with no spare — no reason to carry the risk. **Fix: see the §4 table** (float it, or 1k to GND; and move CSB to 3V3A so the SPI chip select sits released, address 0x76). Whatever is decided, write it into §5.4 — which only mentions PS and CSB.

### (b) LS1 buzzer pin 2 = BUZZ_OUT — **could not be verified (evidence leans correct)**

Full argument in §3.4. What is certain: in the footprint `BUZ-SMD_4P-L8.5-W8.5-P8.50-BR`, pads 1/2 share one edge and pads 3/4 the opposite edge, the four pads are electrically independent in the footprint (no copper joins them), and the symbol names only pin 1 as `+`; the Huaneng datasheet's dimension drawing labels the two pads on one edge `+ Lead / - Lead` and the two on the other (sound-port) edge `Dummy Pad`. **On that basis the drawing is right and leaving LS1.3/LS1.4 floating is right.** And even with 1 ↔ 2 swapped, the MLT-8530 is an electromagnetic coil with unipolar drive, the topology is equivalent, and the only consequence is lower SPL — nothing shorts.

**What cannot be excluded**: those `+ Lead / - Lead / Dummy Pad` labels are vector-graphic text; one fetch could read them and another got only the title page. If that reading is wrong and the corners are really "top two pads = one terminal", the drawing connects 3V3 straight to Q5's drain, shorting the 3V3 rail repeatedly at 2.7 kHz → MCU brown-out reset → §7.4 permanent disarm. **Opening the footprint again cannot solve this (already done; it did not converge).** One multimeter continuity check (coil about 16 ± 3 Ω, dummy pads open) or one supplier terminal drawing settles it. Leave a DNP 0R safety position while undecided.

**Topology and safety properties (re-computed, pass)**

- **Back-to-back P-FET bidirectional blocking holds**: a P-channel body diode conducts from drain to source. Q1 drain = BAT+, source = FET_S: its diode conducts BAT+ → FET_S and blocks FET_S → BAT+; Q2 drain = RAW, source = FET_S: its diode conducts RAW → FET_S and blocks FET_S → RAW. With both off, the forward path BAT+ → RAW is blocked by Q2's diode and the reverse path RAW → BAT+ by Q1's.
- **An open switch means "guaranteed off", not "probably off"**: external switch open → far end of R2 floating → no steady current in R1 (100k) → **Vgs = exactly 0.000 V**, not a high-impedance float. FET_S rises through Q1's body diode to BAT+ − Vsd ≈ 7.7 V, the gate follows through R1, and Vgs stays 0. RAW is held by R3 + R4 = 147 kΩ against Q2's IDSS (≤ 1 µA at 25 °C, ≤ 5 µA at 55 °C), ≤ 0.74 V worst case. Off-state quiescent current is leakage only; the 76 µA path is broken.
- **Gate divider against real AON6403 silicon**: Vgs = −Vbat × 100/110 = **−7.64 V @ 8.4 V / −6.73 V @ 7.4 V / −5.45 V @ 6.0 V**, 2.48× against the worst-case −2.2 V VGS(th), and beyond the −4.5 V RDS(on) specification point (3.5 mΩ typ / 4.3 mΩ max); VGS absolute maximum ±20 V gives 2.62× at −7.64 V. R1 + R2 quiescent current 76.4 µA @ 8.4 V, matching the document.
- **Battery reversal, node by node**: with BAT+ at −8.4 V, Q1's body diode is reverse-biased; FET_S and GATE_SW are pulled to ~0 through R1/R2; both Vgs = 0; Q2's body-diode anode (RAW) ≈ 0 and cathode (FET_S) ≈ 0, not conducting. The bidirectional SMBJ12CA does not conduct at 8.4 V of either polarity. Reverse current is TVS plus FET leakage, microamps. The 470 µF and ceramics on RAW see 0 V; nothing downstream is stressed.
- **Inrush computed (not estimated)**: the gate node's 2 × Ciss = 15.2 nF against R1 ‖ R2 = 9.09 kΩ, τ = 138 µs, |Vgs| crossing the typical threshold at 35 µs. Numerically integrating the FET (gFS = 82 S) and load (984 µF derated, 78.6 mΩ loop) gives a **90 A** peak, against a 107 A resistive upper bound; AON6403 IDM = −280 A, 3.1× margin; peak instantaneous dissipation 226 W for about 30 µs, 7.2 mJ total in the FETs (a share of 34.7 mJ), far inside the DFN5×6 transient SOA at that pulse width; Vds = 90 A × 4.3 mΩ = 0.39 V throughout, so both devices spend almost the whole time in the ohmic region. **"The load switch is not a soft start" holds.** *(GEOMETRY_REV_G §5.7 and SIMULATION.md reach different inrush numbers; SIMULATION.md's SPICE result is the latest.)*
- **Switch contact bounce is filtered out by the gate network** (a useful property nobody had written down): during a momentary switch open, the gate relaxes only through R1 (100k) against 2 × Ciss 15.2 nF, τ = 1.52 ms; |Vgs| stays above −4.5 V for 0.81 ms and above the worst-case −2.2 V threshold for 1.89 ms. Contact interruptions shorter than ~0.8 ms never reach the FETs; longer ones are left to C4's hold-up.
- **The diode OR does what it must**: the complete membership of the `RAW` net is `{C1.1 C2.1 C3.1 D2.2 J4.1 J5.2/5/8/11 Q2.5–Q2.9 R3.1}` — **no VBUS or VLOGIC endpoint at all** — and `J4.1` (ARM terminal A) is on RAW, so with the master switch open PYRO_BUS is dead, exactly what §1's "the pyro bus must be taken after SW" requires. (Strictly, this should be stated as a **leakage budget** rather than as topology: D2's SS34 reverse leakage really can lift a floating RAW, 10 µA × 147 kΩ = 1.47 V. But the conclusion stands — with the ARM plug in, the 11 kΩ ARM_SENSE divider parallels the 147 kΩ down to 10.2 kΩ, leaving ~1 V even at 100 µA of leakage, and Q3/Q4 are held off by their 1 kΩ gate pull-downs, so at any voltage there is no current path through an igniter. **Firing is still impossible.**)
- **The OR also isolates logic hold-up from servo transient loads** (a non-obvious benefit): during a servo stall RAW's own 514 µF feeds the motors and RAW sags; once RAW falls below VLOGIC, D2 blocks, and C4's 470 µF does not have to compete with a 12 A load for charge.
- **The buck does not drop out as the battery sags**: at 60–80 mA of buck input current the SS34 Vf ≈ 0.28 V, so a 6.0 V end-of-charge battery gives VLOGIC = 5.65 V, far from both the 3.8 V recommended VIN minimum and the ~3.06 V falling UVLO. RAW must collapse to 3.85 V (2.1 V per cell) before the buck gives up, by which time the battery is long dead.
- **Power-up / power-down sequencing is satisfied structurally**: DS12110 requires no other supply above VDD + 300 mV while VDD < 1 V, and VDD falling no faster than 10 µs/V. Because L2's 78 mΩ DCR ties 3V3A to 3V3, sustaining a 300 mV difference would take 3.8 A through the inductor — both rails track to millivolts through both transitions. 3V3 falls at about 180 mA / 37 µF = 204 µs/V, 20× slower than 10 µs/V.
- **The LC's attenuation at the switching frequency is real, not just an asymptote**: the two-pole asymptote gives 40·log10(1.1 MHz / fc); checked independently by impedance ratio — at 1.1 MHz ωL = 32.5 Ω, while a 22 µF 0805 is essentially at self-resonance (1/ωC ≈ 9 mΩ against ~1.5 nH of ESL + mounting inductance, ωL ≈ 10 mΩ, Z ≈ ESR ≈ 5 mΩ) — about 76 dB; above self-resonance the divider flattens at L2 / (ESL / 2) ≈ 76 dB, so it does not collapse at the harmonics either.
- **The battery divider against the H7 ADC's published limits**: source resistance 100k ‖ 47k = 31.97 kΩ, 1.56× against DS12110 Table 85's RAIN maximum of 50 kΩ; no capacitor needed as an excuse. Required sampling time RAIN × CADC × ln(2^18) (CADC = 4 pF) = 1.60 µs = 32 cycles at 20 MHz, 25× margin against the H7's 810.5-cycle maximum. C12's 10 nF makes this even less relevant (charge sharing with the 4 pF sampling capacitor drops only 400 ppm, recovering with τ = 320 µs). The three 10k/1k dividers have 909 Ω source resistance, 55× inside the limit.

- **Firing is robust across the whole envelope**: fixed series resistance taken as 0.15 Ω (40 mΩ battery + 20 mΩ board and terminals + 8.6 mΩ Q1 + Q2 + ~15 mΩ 5 A blade fuse + ~20 mΩ plug and harness + 30–65 mΩ RDS(on)): 8.4 V / 1 Ω bridge / 1 m of 26 AWG = **5.92 A**; 8.4 V / 2 Ω = 3.47 A; 6.0 V / 1 Ω = 4.23 A; 6.0 V / 2 Ω / 1 m of 30 AWG = **2.12 A** (worst); 8.4 V / 0.5 Ω low-resistance igniter = 11.2 A. It still protects: a hard short from a pyro output to the bus draws about 84 A and blows in about 5 ms, and it also protects against the assembly error "battery wired to the pyro terminals".
- **No coupling between channels**: firing channel 1 pulls PYRO_BUS down only 5.92 × 0.045 = 0.27 V; channel 2's sense current is set by its own 11 kΩ and is unaffected. No path lets one channel's firing set off the other.
- **The gate networks are entirely on the board**: `PYRO1_GATE_N` / `PYRO2_GATE_N` connect only U1 and R16.1/R20.1; `PYRO1_GATE` / `PYRO2_GATE` connect only resistors, capacitors, pull-downs and FET gates. No gate net leaves the board, so external ESD or RF cannot reach a gate. All four SRV05-4 channels are dedicated to servo signals, none wasted on the pyro block — the right allocation.
- **The J4 terminal assignment matches §4.5/§5.2 word for word**, with the two arming pins at the far end of the terminal strip; **there is no ground on the ARM terminal**, so a single screwdriver slip between adjacent positions cannot complete a firing loop.
- **ARM_SENSE achieves what it was added for**: "armed, no igniter" reads 0.764 V / CONT 0.000 V, "disarmed" reads 0.000 V / 0.000 V — 15165 sixteen-bit codes apart at 8.4 V, still 10832 codes at the 6.0 V end. The problem raised in §1 really is solved.
- **Continuous bridgewire dissipation while armed is negligible**: 0.764 mA into 2 Ω, 1.17 µW × 1800 s = **2.1 mJ** over a 30-minute pad wait.

**Servo chain**

- **The order is right, and it is the half that actually protects the MCU**: on all four channels the 10k pull-down and SRV05-4 channel are on the MCU side and the 220R on the outside (`SERVO1_MCU = {U1.22, R28.1, D5.1, R24.1}`, `SERVO1_OUT` on the other side of the 220R), per §5.3 "the order must not be reversed". Numerically: an IEC 61000-4-2 8 kV contact discharge (330 Ω source, 30 A peak) sees 220 + 330 = 550 Ω, so the peak reaching the clamp and the pin falls to about 14.5 A, within the SRV05-4's capability; with the array on the connector side it would take the full 30 A while the MCU had only the 220R against its residual voltage. **Inside is right**, and §1's correction of the C1 ruling stands.
- **3.3 V through a series resistor drives the servos adequately**, provable from published data rather than experience: the HV6120's published minimum operating voltage is **3.5 V**; a servo that must work from a 3.5 V supply cannot have a 5 V logic rail (no LDO headroom, and micro servos carry no boost), so its internal logic rail is ≤ 3.3 V and VIH ≤ 0.7 × 3.3 = **2.31 V**. Delivered levels (with 220R): 3.28 V @ Rin = 100k, 3.22 V @ 10k, 3.14 V @ 4.7k, worst 1.36×; with 680R, 2.87 V @ 4.7k, still 1.24×.
- **The twelve J5 pins account cleanly, no duplicates, none missing**: 1/4/7/10 the four signals, 2/5/8/11 all RAW, 3/6/9/12 all GND. All six D5 pins used. The four SERVOn_MCU nets are structurally identical (4 endpoints each), each SERVOn_OUT has 2.
- **The PA0–PA3 register rules have datasheet backing**: §7.2's reason for `PUPDR[PA0..3] = 00`, "a pull voids the 7.3 V FT rating", is DS12110 Table 20 note 4 verbatim: "To sustain a voltage higher than 4V the internal pull-up/pull-down resistors must be disabled". Driving push-pull low before switching to AF2/TIM5 at boot is also right, and the four external 10k pull-downs cover the window before the first instruction.
- **Source-current budget is comfortable**: two LEDs ~6 mA + four servo pull-downs 0.33 mA + buzzer gate divider 2.2 mA ≈ 15 mA, against Table 21's ΣI(PIN) of 140 mA.

**USB / debug / clocks**

- **Every easy-to-get-wrong item in the USB-C block is right**: CC1 (J3.A5) and CC2 (J3.B5) **each have their own 5.1k** (R7/R8); the USBLC6 is flow-through with no stubs; all four VBUS contacts paralleled; D+/D− reach PA12/PA11 correctly; no external 1.5k D+ pull-up — the DS12110 USB characteristics table gives "Embedded USB_DP pull-up value during idle RPUI 900/1250/1600 Ω" and "during reception RPUR 1400/2300/3200 Ω", and note 2 of the same table says "No external termination series resistors are required on USB_DP (D+) and USB_DM (D−); the matching impedance is already included in the embedded driver", confirming both of §1's deletions. SBU1/SBU2 don't exist on the symbol and are unconnected, right for a USB 2.0-only receptacle. The OTG_FS load also complies: ES0392 §2.2.12 warns against more than 5 mA on D+/D−, and they drive only the USBLC6 (3.5 pF max, 150 nA leakage per line) and the host's 15 kΩ pull-downs.
- **Full scan against AN2606**: the STM32H74x/75x bootloader touches only PA2, PA3, PA4–PA7, PA8, PA9, PA10, PA15, PB6, PB9, PB10, PB11, PB14, PB15, PC9–PC12, PE11–PE14, PF0, PF1, PH13, PH14. Of this board's digital interfaces only **PB6 and PB9** hit, both in open-drain mode (Table 113: I2C1 SCL on PB6 "open-drain no pull", SDA on PB9), fully compatible with R10's 10k and R12/R13's 4.7k pull-ups — with the DFU jumper in, PB6 is held high and the flash stays deselected. The bootloader's I2C slave address 0b1001110x does not collide with the MS5611's 0x77. SPI1 (PB3/PB4/PB5/PB7), PE10, QSPI (PB2/PD11/PD12/PD13/PE2), USART6 (PC6/PC7), USB (PA11/PA12), SWD (PA13/PA14), PD14 and PD15 are all clean. *(The M3 camera UART later used PB10/PB11 = USART3; this list is why that consequence for DFU was noted.)*
- **PB2 is not an H7 boot pin**: the word "BOOT1" appears nowhere in DS12110 (the H7 uses the BOOT_ADD0/1 option bytes), so the classic F4 trap does not apply.
- **NRST handling is exactly ST's own recommendation**: DS12110 §6.3.16 "It is connected to a permanent pull-up resistor, RPU" of 30/40/50 kΩ, and Figure 22 "Recommended NRST pin protection" shows one capacitor to ground and nothing else. So 100 nF with no external pull-up is right, and §1's deletion of the supervisor, button and external pull-up is justified. No debugger incompatibility (ST-LINK's NRST is open drain and discharges 100 nF instantly; the only pulse requirement is VNF(NRST) ≥ 300 ns, with ≤ 50 ns pulses filtered); reset release is delayed at most 50 kΩ × 100 nF × ln(1/0.3) = 6.0 ms. No interaction with ROM bootloader entry (BOOT0 is a jumper-held DC level, not a timed signal).
- **BOOT0 is right with a huge margin**: BOOT0 has no internal pull and only 15 nA of leakage, 0.15 mV across the 10k pull-down, about 4800× margin against VIL(BOOT0) = 0.19·VDD + 0.1 = 0.727 V. With the jumper in, 3.3 V against VIH(BOOT0) = 0.17·VDD + 0.6 = 1.161 V is 2.8×, adding only 330 µA to 3V3. BOOT0 absolute maximum is 9.0 V, so a stray 5 V source won't damage it. **The rule "no capacitor on BOOT0" is correctly followed** — a charged capacitor could hold BOOT0 above 1.161 V through a brief brown-out and drop a rocket in flight into the ROM bootloader.
- **SWD/DFU header pin order matches §5.5 literally**: 1 = GND, 2 = SWCLK (PA14, 76), 3 = SWDIO (PA13, 72), 4 = NRST (14), 5 = 3V3, 6 = BOOT0 (94). Pins 5 and 6 are adjacent, so a DFU jumper works. SWDIO's internal pull-up and SWCLK's internal pull-down are the SWD idle state; no external resistors needed.
- **HSE connected correctly**: `HSE_IN = {U1.12, Y1.1, C25.1}`, `HSE_OUT = {U1.13, Y1.3, C26.1}`, `Y1.2/Y1.4` grounded — NDK's own note: "#2 and #4 are not connected... It is better to connect with a GND", so grounding is the preferred treatment. No external feedback resistor needed (DS12110 Table 43: RF = 200 kΩ internal). REXT unfitted per §3.1.
- **The pull-pin logic levels themselves are correct with good margin** (the problems are protection and failure direction, not levels): DS12110 Table 59 gives VIL max = 0.3·VDD, VIH min = 0.7·VDD. Pin in: 3.3 × 1k/11k = **0.300 V** against VIL 0.99 V (0.285 V against 0.94 V at VDD − 5 %), 3.3×. Pin out: 3.30 V against VIH 2.31 V, 1.43×; ±1 µA of leakage across the 10k adds only ~10 mV. It even survives a firmware mistake: if PD14's internal pull-up is enabled by mistake, RPU 30k in parallel with 10k = 7.5k and the pin-in level is 0.388 V, still inside VIL. The typical 250 mV input hysteresis helps the transition.
- **The buzzer gate drive is fine on its own** (the same divider that hurts the pyros is harmless here): Vgs = 2.245 V, 0.8 V of overdrive against the 1.45 V worst-case VGS(th); RDS(on), extrapolated from 48 mΩ max at 2.5 V, is about 55–60 mΩ — only 11 mV and about 1 mW at the 185 mA peak. Switching: the 470 ‖ 1000 = 320 Ω drive Thevenin into Ciss 630 pF gives 0.2 µs edges, 0.11 % of a 185 µs half-period. `BUZZ_GATE_N → R34 → BUZZ_GATE → R35 → GND` is connected correctly.
- **The MLT-8530 is confirmed passive**, as in the frozen document: the Huaneng datasheet is titled "Specification for Electro-Magnetic Buzzer (SMD Type)", shows a "Recommended Driving Circuit" with an external transistor, says "Resonant frequency, 1/2 duty cycle. Square wave. Signal amplitude should be large enough to saturate the transistor", and publishes a coil resistance (16 ± 3 Ω) and resonant frequency (2700 Hz) rather than an operating current at some DC voltage — all hallmarks of an externally driven transducer.
- **Ample 3V3 budget while the buzzer sounds**: MCU at 200 MHz ~150 mA + SAM-M10Q acquisition ~30 mA + W25Q128 page program 25 mA + IMU/barometer 2 mA + two LEDs 12 mA + buzzer peak 185 mA ≈ **404 mA**, 5× headroom against the AP63203's 2 A.
- **VCAP correct and not shared**: C19 on U1.48 and C20 on U1.73, two separate 2.2 µF, per Table 24 (CEXT = 2.2 µF, ±20 % acceptable, ESR < 100 mΩ); the chosen CL10A225KO8NNNC X5R 0603 is 10–30 mΩ at 1 MHz, inside the limit.
- **MCU decoupling matches the DS12110 supply scheme**: N × 100 nF (N = 5 VDD pins) + 1 × 4.7 µF. VBAT (pin 6) copper-tied to 3V3 with its own 100 nF, explicitly allowed by DS12110 §3.19 ("The voltage on the VBAT pin could be provided by an external battery, a supercapacitor or directly by VDD").
- **No output contention**: all 78 nets scanned; none has two potential drivers. SPI1_MISO has one source (the IMU's AP_SDO, tri-stated when CS is high); both I2C1 lines are open drain at every endpoint; QSPI is single master, single slave, gated by /CS; USB D+/D− is a half-duplex differential pair; SWDIO is arbitrated by protocol; BOOT0's 10k pull-down against the 3V3 jumper is an intentional 330 µA load, not contention. Apart from those named in this report there is no undefined floating MCU input (`SPARE_ADC` / PB0 only goes to the unfitted J8 and sits in the H7's analog reset state, the right state for an unused pin).
- **Part integrity**: 104 parts, 370 connections; every pin of every part is either on a net or has an explicit reason not to be. C27 and R6 are reference gaps, not orphan parts with floating pins — no part in the netlist has an unaccounted pin.
- **The passive budget matches §4.2 part by part** (strong circumstantial evidence that the reference-to-value mapping is the design intent): resistors 13 × 10k (R5 BOOT0, R10 NCS, R11 IMU_CS, R36 pull-pin, R14/R18/R22 divider upper arms, R28–R31 servo pull-downs, R35, R2 SW gate pull), 6 × 1k, 3 × 100k, 1 × 47k, 2 × 5.1k, 2 + 2 × 4.7k, 6 × 220R, 3 × 470R = **38, matching the 38 drawn**; capacitors 14 × 100 nF + 2 × 10 pF + 3 × 10 nF + 1 × 1 µF + 2 × 2.2 µF + 2 × 4.7 µF + 9 × 22 µF + 2 × 470 µF = **35, matching the 35 drawn** (the missing 100 nF is C27 in the §4 table). The distribution of 10 on 3V3 and 9 on 3V3A also confirms the rail split.
- **The frozen document's own quantitative results all reproduce from the drawn topology**, cross-validating the resistor identification: Vgs −7.636 V @ 8.4 V / −5.455 V @ 6.0 V, SW terminal 76.4 µA (§1/§2.2 say −7.64 / −5.45 / 76 µA); VBAT_SENSE 2.686 V @ 8.4 V / 1.918 V @ 6.0 V (§3.3); PYRO_CONT normal 0.764 V, 3.273 V at a 36 V avalanche, 3.636 V at 40 V, 909 Ω source (§1/§3.3); ARM_SENSE 1.809 V at the 19.9 V clamp (§3.3); bridgewire current 0.632 → 0.764 mA with the divider change (§1).
- **The "10k/3.3k vs 33k/10k contradiction in §7.5" does not exist**: `grep '33k'` has zero hits; `3.3k` appears once in the whole text, in §1's change table as the **superseded** original spec; §7 is fourteen flat items with no §7.5 subsection. §1, §3.3, §5.2 and §4.2 agree on 10k/1k, and the arithmetic confirms 10k/1k is the electrically right choice for both dividers: 10k/3.3k gives 8.93 V in a 36 V avalanche (over FT's 7.3 V absolute maximum), and its 2.48 kΩ Rth would force 2.27 mA of positive injection into a pin rated 0 mA. (The one honest reservation: as drawn, 10k/1k gives 3.636 V at the **40 V** worst corner, 0.34 V above VDDA, injecting at most (3.636 − 3.3)/909 = 0.37 mA into the analog switch for the duration of the avalanche and clipping the reading at full scale — acceptable for a present/absent decision, but it is the one place in the whole design sitting right at its own margin, and it should be written down rather than left implicit.)
