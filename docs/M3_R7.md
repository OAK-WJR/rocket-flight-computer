### Antenna bias limiter

The topology is u-blox's PNP / P-FET limiter (pass1_electrical §6.1):
- VCC_RF feeds R300 (15 Ω sense), then Q30 (AO3401A switch), then L30 (47 nH bias tap), then the antenna.
- Q31 (AO3400A) turns Q30 on through R302 while LNA_EN is high. With LNA_EN low, R301 holds Q30 off.
- Q32 (S8550) senses the R300 drop and pulls Q30's gate up, which limits the current. No firmware is
  involved.

### RF chains

- **GNSS:** J30 → D30 (ESD103, ±15 V working, which clears the 3.2 V bias) → L30 bias tap (C302 10 nF at
  its cold end) → C303 shunt (DNP) → R304 0 Ω → C305 shunt (DNP) → U30 RF_IN.
  - The bias sits on the antenna side, so R304 can become a series capacitor without cutting the feed.
  - C303, and C305 while R304 is 0 Ω, sit on the DC bias node: anything fitted there must be capacitive
    (ORDERING §4). C304 takes a capacitor only (1.0–2.2 nF): an inductor or short there holds Q30's gate at GND,
    which removes the current limit.
  - The 0.94 mm traces from each shunt land to its series pad (C303, C305, C313, C314) are part of the network:
    include them in the VNA tuning model (pass-2 RF-02's abutting of the lands was not done; P3-RF-03).
  - A shunt inductor in either position would short the antenna feed. The limiter makes that harmless,
    but the antenna goes dark.
  - **ESD trade-off.** D30 is the same ESD103 as D31 (one extended code at the 52-code limit; step A). It
    clamps at 20, 36 and 48 V at 1, 8 and 16 A TLP, far above pass 1's TPD1E0B04, and the residual reaches
    U30 RF_IN through R304; u-blox gives RF_IN ±5.5 V DC and no ESD figure. Handling rule instead: connect
    or disconnect the GNSS antenna only unpowered on an ESD-safe bench, never hot-plug it in the field
    (ORDERING §5, §7). If an extended code is ever freed (R300 split, below), TPD1E0B04DPYR (C1975594) can go
    at D30 (`pass2_electrical.md` ELECTRICAL-04).
- **Radio:** J31 → D31 (ESD103: the +22 dBm peaks are about 4.1 V, and about 7.5 V at 10:1 VSWR) →
  C313 shunt (DNP) → R316 0 Ω → C314 shunt (DNP) → U31 ANT.
  - There is no L31: the pass-1 DC-to-ground shunt was dropped by the step-A adjudication.
- **The DNP shunts are tuning placeholders for VNA work** (dnp, excluded from the BOM and from position
  files). `tools/export_fab.py` now reports them as unfitted placeholders and never lists them as
  owner-fitted. A DNP part left in the BOM without a part number stops the export (`tools/test_fab_validation.py`).
  They and C304 use `rocket:C0402_DNP` (C0402 copper, mask and courtyard, no paste), so JLC prints no solder
  on the empty lands; `check_r7.dnp_paste` fails any DNP part with paste.
- **RF grounds:** the GND pads of D30, D31, C303, C305, C313 and C314 connect solid to the pour, each with a GND
  via 0.16 mm from its pad edge (`check_r7.rf_grounds`). C302.2 (the bias tap's cold-end capacitor, behind L30)
  went back to the house thermal relief in fix pass 4 (`r7_polish.rf_relief`; pour contact 0.111 → 0.045 mm²; its
  GND via stays; `check_r7.RF_SOLID_GND` no longer lists it, so `thermal_policy` requires the relief). **D30.2 and
  D31.2 stay solid** (fix-pass-4 decision): the ESD clamp's return inductance matters at the jack, and a spoke adds
  it, while the tombstoning risk of unequal heating is a general-practice inference with no maker figure (pass-3
  DFM). The first-article inspection checks D30, D31 and C302 seated flat, with no tombstone or skew (ORDERING §5;
  P3-RF-02, P3-DFM-05, P4-RF-03).

−3.7 % (fix-pass-3 grid study). Read the finest grid: track-limited paths are good to about ±5 % (only two
significant figures carry information), the others to about ±2 %, so differences smaller than that between boards
are not significant.

### High-current copper: IPC-2152 estimate (P5-DOCS-02)

AGENTS §3 and REQUIREMENTS M2 ask for high-current copper sized per IPC-2152; F4 bounds the servo feed at 7.7 A
(four servos stalled, ≤ 1.92 A each). Estimate on the final board (`c872a3d3…`) by F8, with the method the fix-pass-5
records agent set up, at the ordered copper (1 oz = 35 µm outer, 30 µm inner, JLC041611-7628, M1). Fix pass 7 reworked
the three necks within bounds (stage `necks7`, no part moved; deviation C1).

- **Method.** `tools/dc_solve.py`'s current field for each 7.7 A path at 0.05 mm, cut along about 375 equipotentials
  (3–97 % of the drop; marching squares per layer). At each cut the copper length on each layer and the current it
  carries give the width that carries the whole 7.7 A there; the narrowest cut is the neck. Script: the session
  scratchpad's `f8/dc_ipc.py` (a copy of `dc_solve.py` with the cut analysis appended; the fix-pass-5 records' was
  `rec5/ipc/dc_ipc.py`), which reproduces the records' 0.05 mm necks on the fix-pass-6 board exactly; at 0.1 mm the
  final necks are RAW_ACT 2.95, ACT_FET_S 2.64, BAT_IN 3.66 mm. One RAW_ACT cut at 97 % of the drop (0.5 mm, a contour
  round Q121's via field) is not counted, as the via fields are not covered (below).
- **Temperature rise.** Brooks & Adam's fits to the IPC-2152 charts, as KiCad's track-width calculator implements
  them (`TRACK_WIDTH_CALCULATIONS`; D. Brooks and J. Adam, "Trace Current/Temperature Relationships", PCD&F, 29 May
  2015): ΔT = K · I^a · W^b · Th^c with W and Th in mil; external K 215.3, a 2.0, b −1.15, c −1.0; internal (1 oz
  class, which 30 µm falls in) K 200, a 1.9, b −1.10, c −1.52. They describe IPC-2152's test conditions: a long single
  conductor on a polyimide board with no copper plane, so they are an upper bound for a short neck inside a wide pour
  (Inference: heat spreads into the adjoining copper). Brooks & Adam's simulations of their control trace give the
  plane and material corrections used as ratios below: a plane 10 mil under the trace 0.36 × (94.9 → 33.7 °C), a plane
  on the far side of a 63 mil board 0.50 ×, FR-4 instead of polyimide 1.29 × (Inference: one control case scaled, not
  a simulation of this board). Here F.Cu has solid In1 GND 0.203 mm (8 mil) below and B.Cu the In2 pours 0.203 mm
  above (× 0.36 × 1.29 ≈ 0.46); In2's nearest solid plane is In1 GND 1.03 mm away (× 0.50 × 1.29 ≈ 0.65).
